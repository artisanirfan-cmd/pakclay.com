## What decides the price of khaprail tiles?

**The price of a khaprail tile is set by its size and profile, its finish, the order quantity and the distance it travels, and it moves with fuel and labour costs through the year.** Here is each factor in plain terms.

**Size and profile.** A larger or more complex profile uses more clay and more kiln space. Spanish and French curved profiles are shaped and fired differently from flat rectangular tiles, so their costs differ.

**Finish.** Natural red clay is the base. Glazed tiles need an extra glazing and firing step, which usually puts them in a higher bracket than unglazed tiles.

**Quantity.** Roofing is bought in thousands of pieces. Larger orders usually allow better terms, and a small repair batch is priced differently from a full villa roof.

**Delivery.** Tiles are heavy and fragile. Distance, vehicle, loading and packing all add to the landed cost, so a quote for Lahore delivery and a quote for Karachi or Peshawar delivery are not the same thing.

**Season and inputs.** Kiln fuel, labour and transport costs change, and so do rates. Demand also rises before and after the monsoon, when many owners repair or build.

**Quality of firing.** A cheaper tile that cracks in the first monsoon is the most expensive tile you can buy. Compare the quality of the tile, not only the figure.

## How should you ask for a khaprail price?

Ask every supplier the same questions so the answers can be compared:

1. Is the rate per piece or per square foot of roof covered?
2. Which profile and finish is it for? (Spanish, French, flat, glazed or natural.)
3. How many pieces will my roof need, including a margin for breakage?
4. Does the rate include delivery to my site and unloading?
5. What is the date of the quote, and how long is it valid?
6. Can I see and handle a sample first?

Send your city, the approximate roof area and the profile you like, and a good supplier can respond with a figure you can actually plan around.

## Is clay roofing worth the cost compared with other roofing?

Clay tile is usually bought for its long working life, its thermal comfort and its look rather than for the lowest first cost. Metal sheets and concrete tiles can cost less at the start. Over decades, a well-fired clay tile that does not need replacing can come out ahead, and clay does not rust or need repainting. The honest answer depends on your budget, how long you will own the property and the style you want. We would rather you decide with real numbers than with a slogan.

## How do city and delivery change the rate?

Pakistan is large and tiles are heavy. A truckload to Lahore does not cost the same to move as one to Karachi, Peshawar or Quetta, and loose or poorly packed loads break more. When you compare quotes, ask each supplier whether the rate is ex-works or delivered to your site, and who pays for breakage in transit. A low rate that excludes transport can end up higher than a delivered quote.

## What mistakes make quotes misleading?

- **No unit.** "Rs X" means nothing without per piece, per square foot or per thousand.
- **No profile named.** Flat, Spanish and French tiles are not interchangeable.
- **No date.** Rates move, so an old figure is only a rumour.
- **A rate for the tile only,** with laying, ridge pieces and transport added later.
- **Unseen goods.** Always ask for a sample from the batch you will actually receive.

## Which factors should you rank first?

For most home builders the order is: firing quality, then profile fit for the roof pitch, then delivered cost, then finish. A glazed colour may be worth extra on a feature roof, while a plain natural-red tile suits a large, budget-conscious roof. Architects and contractors buying in bulk should ask for a quote on the whole quantity at once.
