## What is terracotta jali, and where does it belong in a Pakistani home?

Stand in an old haveli courtyard or a mosque screen in Lahore and you will see light falling through a pattern of small openings, throwing a moving lattice onto the floor. That is the idea behind jali: a perforated screen that lets air and light pass while softening the view. Terracotta jali brings the same idea to modern houses, in fired clay with a warm red colour.

If you are searching for terracotta jali in Pakistan, this guide explains what it is, the main places it works, how to choose a design, and how to order sensibly.

---

## What is terracotta jali?

**Terracotta jali is a perforated clay unit, either a tile or a block, used to build screens, partitions and decorative wall openings that allow air and light to pass.** The word jali comes from the Urdu and Hindi for a net or lattice, and the tradition of pierced screens in stone and clay is centuries old across South Asia.

You will see it described as jali tiles, jaali, clay jali or terracotta lattice. In practice the unit comes in two broad forms: thinner tile-like pieces that are fixed in a frame or on a wall, and thicker block-like pieces that are stacked to build a screen. Ask a supplier which form they mean, because the way you fix them is different.

## Where can you use terracotta jali?

Typical uses in Pakistani homes and buildings include:

- **Boundary and garden walls.** A band of jali near the top of a wall lets air through and breaks up a solid face.
- **Balcony and terrace screens.** It gives some privacy while keeping the space airy.
- **Staircase and corridor walls.** Light enters, and the pattern becomes a feature.
- **Kitchen and utility walls.** Ventilation can help where heat and smell collect.
- **Room dividers.** A freestanding or half-height screen separates a lounge from a dining area without closing the room.
- **Facades.** A jali front over a window or balcony can reduce direct glare, though results depend on orientation and pattern.

Jali does not keep out rain, so a screen on the outside of a building is not a substitute for a wall or window where you need weather protection.

## How do you choose a jali design?

Choose in this order.

**Purpose first.** Decide whether you want ventilation, privacy, light or simply a look. A boundary wall band, a balcony and a kitchen all weigh these differently.

**Opening size.** Large openings give more airflow and more view through. Small openings give privacy and a finer shadow. If you are unsure, compare a sample against the actual view you want to screen.

**Pattern scale.** A large pattern on a small wall can look crowded, while a tiny pattern on a large facade can look busy from a distance. Hold the sample up where it will go.

**Orientation.** A west-facing screen takes harsh afternoon sun, and the shadow pattern moves through the day. Think about when the space is used.

**Maintenance.** Open patterns collect dust and can attract insects, so a design that is easy to brush or wash suits kitchens and exterior walls better than a very intricate one.

## How is terracotta jali installed?

Installation depends on the form and the span. As general practice, thin jali tiles are fixed in a frame or set in mortar with consistent joints, while stacked blocks are laid course by course, usually with a structural frame or reinforcement for larger areas. Unsupported tall screens can be fragile, so ask a mason or engineer to design anything larger than a small panel.

A few habits help on site: keep a straight line with a string, check each course for level, keep mortar out of the openings as you work, and clean the face before the mortar sets. Order a few extra pieces, since clay is breakable and cutting wastes some units.

## Is terracotta jali better than cement or metal jali?

It depends on the project, and each has a place.

| Material | Typical strength | Typical drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Terracotta | Warm natural colour, traditional look | Breakable, needs support in large spans |
| Cement | Can be made in many shapes and sizes | Heavier, colour is usually grey unless finished |
| Metal | Slim and strong, wide spans | Heats up in the sun, can corrode |

If you like the colour of fired clay and the traditional look, terracotta is a natural choice. If your design needs a very large span or an unusual shape, ask whether cement or metal makes more sense.

## What should you check before ordering?

- **See the real piece.** Photos help, but a sample shows colour, surface and edge quality.
- **Check dimensions** and thickness on the sample, not only on a list.
- **Count the pieces** for your wall area, plus extra for breakage.
- **Name the use**, so the supplier can say whether the piece suits it.
- **Plan the support.** Ask a mason how larger screens should be framed.
- **Get a written, dated quote** with the unit and inclusions.
