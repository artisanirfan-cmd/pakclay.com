## What colours do khaprail tiles come in, and how do you pick one for your roof?

Ask ten people what colour a khaprail roof is and nine will say red. That is the traditional answer, and it comes from the clay itself. But roof colour is also one of the first decisions a homeowner makes, and one of the hardest to undo once the tiles are on. This guide explains the khaprail tile colours Pak Clay lists today, why natural clay and glazed tiles look so different, how light and weather change what you see, and how to choose with a real sample in your hand.

## What are khaprail tiles?

**Khaprail tiles are fired clay roofing tiles, made by shaping natural clay and baking it in a kiln until hard. They are the Punjab's traditional sloped-roof covering and the local relative of terracotta roof tiles.** The colour of an unglazed tile comes from the clay and the firing, while a glazed tile gets its colour from a coating fired onto the surface.

## Why does natural red look different from glazed green?

The two finishes behave differently, and that affects how they age.

- **Natural red clay** is colour-through, meaning the colour runs through the body of the tile rather than sitting on the surface. Edge chips and scratches tend to show the same colour underneath. The tone depends on the clay and the firing, so some variation from tile to tile is normal and often considered part of the charm.
- **Glazed tiles** have a fired-on coating. The surface is smoother, tends to wipe clean more easily, and gives a more uniform colour. A chip through the glaze can show the clay body beneath it.

Neither is better in every situation. Natural red blends with brick, stone and timber, while glazed green makes the roof a deliberate feature.

## Does khaprail colour change over time?

In general, natural clay tiles weather gently. Surface dust, rain and sun can soften the tone over the years, and shaded roofs can gather moss or marks in damp spots. Glazed colour tends to hold its look longer on the surface, though no coating is permanent and we do not publish a fade-rate figure.

The honest advice is to put samples outdoors for a few weeks before you decide. Rinse them, dry them, and look again. That shows you more than a catalogue can.

## How do light and surroundings change what you see?

Colour on a screen is unreliable. Photos are taken in different light, phone screens are set differently, and a tile that looks orange at noon can look deep red in late afternoon. Before choosing:

1. **Look at the sample in your own light**, morning and evening, on a sunny and a cloudy day.
2. **Hold it against your wall colour**, gate, window frames and boundary wall.
3. **View it from a distance.** A roof is seen from the street, not from arm's length.
4. **Place several tiles side by side** to see the batch variation before you commit.

## How do you choose a colour for different house styles?

Some pointers, with the caveat that taste decides:

- **Traditional or heritage-style homes:** natural red usually sits comfortably with brick, plaster and timber.
- **Modern, plain-walled homes:** a flat profile in natural red gives a quiet, clean line, while glazed green offers contrast.
- **Garden houses and farmhouses:** green can echo planting, while red tends to ground a building in its landscape.
- **Neighbourhood rules:** some housing societies or sellers have preferences for roof appearance, so check with your society if it applies to you.

## What should you check before ordering?

- **Order a sample** from the batch you will receive, and look at it in daylight.
- **Order the whole job together** so the colour matches across the roof.
- **Add a margin** for ridges, hips, cuts and breakage, and keep a few spares for repairs.
- **Confirm the colour in writing** on the quote, including finish (natural or glazed) and profile.
- **Agree transport and packing**, and what happens if pieces break on the way.
