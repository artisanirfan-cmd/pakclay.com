## Is cement jali the right screen for your wall, balcony or boundary?

Walk through any Lahore neighbourhood and you will see them: perforated screens on balcony edges, stair landings, boundary walls and house fronts, letting air through while keeping the street at a polite distance. Many of these are cement jali. If you are searching for cement jali or concrete jali in Pakistan, this guide explains what it is, where it works, where it does not, and what to settle before you place an order.

---

## What is cement jali?

**Cement jali is a perforated panel or block made from cast cement or concrete, with repeated openings that let air and light pass while partly screening the view.** The word "jali" comes from the traditional pierced screens of South Asian architecture, once carved from stone. Cast cement made the idea cheaper to repeat, so the same pattern can be produced again and again in a mould.

"Cement jali" and "concrete jali" are used almost interchangeably in the market. If you are comparing quotes, ask what the piece is actually made of and how it was cured, because that affects strength and finish.

## Where is cement jali used?

Common uses include:

- **Balcony and terrace edges,** for privacy with airflow.
- **Boundary and compound walls,** as a decorative upper band above a solid base.
- **Stair and corridor screens,** to bring in daylight without direct glare.
- **Façade panels,** to break up a plain wall and give the house a pattern.
- **Utility areas,** where a screen hides AC units or water tanks but still ventilates them.

These are typical applications, not guarantees. How well a screen performs depends on the opening size, the wall behind it and the climate on your plot.

## What designs and finishes are available?

Patterns usually fall into a few families: geometric grids, floral or star motifs, brick-style staggered openings, vertical slits and plain square holes. Lighter, more open patterns suit a small balcony, while denser patterns give more privacy. Finishes commonly include natural grey, white or lightly textured faces, and some can be painted afterwards.

## Cement jali or terracotta jali: which should you pick?

The honest answer is that they suit different jobs.

| Factor | Cement / concrete jali | Terracotta jali |
|---|---|---|
| Look | Grey or white, plain and modern | Warm fired-clay colour, traditional |
| Weight | Generally heavier per piece | Often lighter, but depends on thickness |
| Shapes | Easy to cast in large or custom moulds | Limited by what can be fired |
| Surface | Can be painted or plastered | Natural colour, usually left unpainted |
| Maintenance | Needs sound joints and waterproofing | Needs sound joints; clay is porous |

If you want the warm colour of fired clay, read our [terracotta jali guide](/blog/terracotta-jali-in-pakistan-designs-uses-and-ordering-guide), which covers designs and fixing. If your plan calls for a larger span, an unusual shape or a painted finish, cement may be the more practical material. Many houses use both: terracotta on the front and cement for the boundary.

## How is cement jali installed?

Installation differs for thin tiles and stacked blocks, so use your mason's judgement and ask an engineer for anything beyond a small panel. As general practice:

1. Mark the opening and check the base is level.
2. Lay blocks course by course in mortar, keeping joints even.
3. For taller or longer screens, include a structural frame, reinforcing or tie-ins as designed by an engineer.
4. Finish the joints neatly, and think about how rain will run off the top edge.

Unsupported tall screens can be fragile. A balcony screen also adds weight at the slab edge, so confirm the structure can carry it before you buy.

## Does cement jali need sealing or painting?

Cement is porous and can stain or darken if it stays damp. Many owners apply a sealer or a coat of exterior paint to even out the colour and make cleaning easier. Joints and the top edge are where water usually gets in, so protect those first. ## What should you check before ordering?

- **Pattern and opening size** in a real sample, held up to the light.
- **Thickness and weight** of the actual piece.
- **Structure:** the slab, wall or frame that will carry the screen.
- **Water path:** how rain leaves the top and joints.
- **Spares:** buy extra from the same batch, as colour varies between pours.
- **A written quote** with unit, inclusions and date.
