## What are terracotta jali tiles?

**Terracotta jali tiles are perforated fired-clay units with repeating openings, set into a wall or frame so that air and filtered light pass through while direct views are partly blocked.** Pak Clay, a Lahore clay tile maker since 1982, offers terracotta jali in several patterns, from fine square grids to circles, scallops and star shapes, listed in the [product catalogue](/products).

## Why use jali on a boundary wall in Lahore?

A solid wall blocks wind and can trap heat on the garden side. A jali section lets breeze pass through, so a lawn or courtyard may feel less closed in. It also softens the look of a long plain wall. Because the pattern is open, you see a broken, filtered view instead of a flat surface. Typical choices include a jali band above a solid base, a jali panel next to a gate, or a lower wall that lets light into a front garden. How much privacy you get depends on the pattern: a fine grid hides more than a large circle, and your viewing distance changes the effect. Look at a sample from the street side, not only up close.

## Which jali patterns suit which position?

Think in terms of the job the opening does.

- **Fine grids.** A small square grid, like the one in [this terracotta jali design](/products/terracotta-jali-tiles-012), tends to give more screening for a garden or a front lawn.
- **Larger circular or scalloped openings.** These suit decorative panels, stairwell walls or a feature wall, where airflow and looks matter more than strict privacy.
- **Star and geometric patterns.** These work as accents in a courtyard or above a gate, and cast patterned shadows when the sun is low.
- **Hollow rectangular blocks.** Some designs are built as blocks that stack into a curved or straight wall, which suits a longer boundary run.

Check the exact size and thickness of the piece you choose before you plan the wall layout, because the spacing of the mortar joints depends on it.

## Can jali be used for ventilation inside the house?

Yes, in places where you want air to move and a solid wall is not needed. Common spots are a staircase void, a store room, a kitchen outer wall, a rooftop terrace screen and a verandah wall. A jali opening is not a replacement for a window if you need to keep out rain, dust or insects, and a fixed jali does not seal. Many people pair a jali screen with a fine mesh or a glazed frame behind it when they need protection from insects or wind-driven rain. For heat control, a screen that shades a wall from direct afternoon sun can help, though the effect depends on orientation and layout.

## Is terracotta jali strong enough for a wall?

A jali wall has more openings than a solid one, so it behaves differently. Clay is strong in compression but a perforated section carries load differently from a solid brick course. We are not structural engineers and we cannot say what height or span is safe for your site. For a boundary wall, a common approach is a solid masonry base and pillars with jali panels set between them, and it is sensible to ask your mason or engineer to confirm pillar spacing and height. Wind exposure, soil and local by-laws all matter in Lahore. Do not treat any catalogue photo as a structural drawing.

## How does terracotta jali cope with Lahore's weather?

Fired clay is a long-established outdoor material and handles sun well. In monsoon, a vertical jali panel sheds most water, but rain can enter the openings and wet the wall behind it. If something sits behind the screen that must stay dry, plan a drip edge, a sloping sill or a protective layer. Clay is porous, so you may see darker patches after rain that fade as it dries. Over years, dust, moss and algae can build up in shaded, damp spots, and a soft brush and water usually clear surface growth. Cleaning with strong acids can mark clay, so test a small area first.

## Terracotta jali or cement jali: which should you choose?

Terracotta has a warm, natural clay colour and a slightly irregular, handmade look. Cement jali is moulded from cement and can be made in different strengths, finishes and sizes, and it is usually painted or left grey. Neither is automatically better. Terracotta suits natural, earthy designs; cement can suit a painted or modern look. Compare by looking at real samples, the thickness, and the finish you want. ## What should you check before ordering jali?

Work through a short list. Measure the length and height of the opening and add a margin for breakage and cuts. Choose the pattern from a physical sample, viewed from the distance people will actually see it. Confirm the piece size and thickness in writing. Ask whether the whole quantity comes from one batch, because clay colour can vary slightly between firings. Decide how the panels will be fixed and ask your mason to plan the joints. Finally, plan cleaning access, especially for panels at height.
