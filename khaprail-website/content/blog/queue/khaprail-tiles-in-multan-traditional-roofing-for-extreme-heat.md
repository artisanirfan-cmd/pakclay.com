## What are khaprail tiles?

**Khaprail tiles are fired clay roofing tiles, shaped from natural clay and baked in a kiln until hard. They are a traditional sloped-roof covering in Punjab and a close relative of terracotta roof tiles.** A khaprail roof sheds rain along its curves or flats and adds a layer of fired clay above the structure.

Clay is a dense mineral material, and a tiled roof tends to feel different from bare concrete in strong sun. How much cooler a house actually feels depends on the full roof build: slope, underlay, ventilation, insulation and ceiling height. We do not publish a temperature drop, and you should be wary of any seller that does.

## How does Multan's climate affect the choice of roof tile?

Multan generally has very hot, long summers, mild winters, low rainfall for most of the year and occasional dust storms and monsoon showers. That suggests a few priorities:

- **Even firing.** Tiles that are fired evenly tend to handle repeated heating and cooling better than patchy ones.
- **Colour.** Natural red looks at home in the city's brick and clay streetscape. Lighter or glazed finishes reflect more sunlight, but the effect varies and should be judged on a real sample.
- **Ventilation.** A gap under the tiles and an airy roof space help release heat. Ask your contractor how the roof will breathe.
- **Dust.** Dusty air settles on tiles, and a gentle wash after storms keeps colour and drainage clear.

No tile solves heat alone; the build around it matters as much as the tile. Our broader [guide to roof tiles for heat in Pakistan](/blog/best-roof-tiles-for-heat-in-pakistan-keeping-homes-cooler) covers this in more depth.

## Do khaprail tiles suit modern concrete roofs in Multan?

Many houses in Multan have flat reinforced concrete roofs. Khaprail tiles are often laid on a sloped structure, so a flat slab may need a sloped frame or a sloped build-up before tiling. This is a structural question, so ask an engineer or an experienced roofing contractor before ordering. Do not guess the load or the slope, and do not accept a promise without a drawing or a site visit.

## What should you check before you order?

- **Ask for a sample** from the batch you will receive, and view it in strong afternoon light.
- **Tap-test a few pieces.** A clear ring usually points to an evenly fired body, a dull sound to a flaw.
- **Check size consistency** by stacking several tiles.
- **Confirm packing and transport**, and what happens to broken pieces.
- **Agree the laying method** with your contractor: slope, overlap, fixing and ventilation.
- **Get the quote in writing**, with unit, inclusions and date.

## How do you look after a clay roof in a dusty, hot city?

A clay roof needs modest care. After dust storms or heavy monsoon showers, check that gutters and valleys are clear, because blocked drainage sends water where it should not go. Use a soft brush or a low-pressure rinse rather than harsh scraping, and look for cracked or slipped tiles from the ground or a safe access point, not by walking over the tiles. Replace broken pieces early, since a single gap can let water into the structure below. Keep a few spare tiles from your original batch, as colour can vary a little between batches.

## Are glazed or natural tiles better for Multan?

Neither wins outright. Natural red has the traditional look and a matte surface, while a glazed finish such as the green option gives a different appearance and a smoother face. Glazed surfaces may reflect more sunlight, but the real effect depends on colour and the roof build, so judge both on a sample placed in direct afternoon sun. Ask about the care each finish needs, and choose the one you will be happy to see for years rather than the one that sounds cooler on paper.
