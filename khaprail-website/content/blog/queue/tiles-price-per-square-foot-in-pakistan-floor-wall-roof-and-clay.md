## What decides the tiles price per square foot in Pakistan?

Search for "tiles price per square foot" and you will find lists of numbers that mix units, cities and years. One page quotes ceramic per square metre, the next quotes granite per square foot, and few say when the rate was collected. That makes a comparison close to useless. This guide takes a different route: it explains how tile rates are built in Pakistan, what changes them, and how to compare quotes fairly, covering floor, wall, outdoor, roof, stone and clay tiles. We do not print a rate here, because a price without a unit and a date can mislead you into the wrong budget.

## What is the tiles price per square foot?

**The tiles price per square foot is the cost of covering one square foot of floor, wall or roof with a given tile, but the figure only means something when it states the material, size, finish, city, date and whether fixing is included.** The same tile can be quoted per piece, per square foot, per square metre, per box or per thousand, so always convert to one unit before comparing.

## Why do tile rates in Pakistan keep changing?

Tile rates move for reasons outside the tile itself. Fuel and gas costs affect firing, transport and kiln work. Imported tiles follow exchange rates, duties and shipping. Construction material rates today can differ from last month's, which is why a printed rate list goes stale quickly. Local demand also matters: rates often shift around building seasons and after rain, when work slows or restarts. Treat any number you see online as a rough signal, never as an order price. A supplier's dated, written quote is the only figure you can budget against.

## How do floor tiles, wall tiles and roof tiles differ in cost?

They are priced against different jobs, so they are not interchangeable.

| Tile type | Usually sold by | Main cost drivers |
|---|---|---|
| Floor tiles (indoor) | Square foot or box | Material, size, finish, thickness |
| Outdoor tiles | Square foot or piece | Grip, frost and sun resistance, thickness |
| Wall tiles | Square foot or box | Size, glaze, pattern, trim pieces |
| Roof tiles | Piece or thousand | Profile, firing quality, accessories like ridge pieces |
| Stone tiles | Square foot | Stone type, cutting, thickness, weight |

Roof tiles are the odd one out. Khaprail roof tiles are normally counted by the piece, so a "per square foot" figure for a roof depends on how many pieces cover a given area, which in turn depends on profile and overlap. Ask your supplier for the piece count per area before you convert anything.

## What makes clay and terracotta tiles cost what they do?

Clay tiles are made from fired clay, so their cost follows the process: raw material, shaping, drying, firing and handling. Handmade and hand-finished pieces take more labour than machine-pressed ones. Size and thickness change the amount of clay and the breakage risk. Shape matters too, since hexagons, octagons and star-and-cross pieces need more cutting and sorting than plain squares. Each is priced a different way. Stone cladding depends on the stone, the cut face and weight. Jali screens are usually counted per piece because the pattern and thickness vary. ## What extra costs sit outside the tile rate?

This is where budgets slip. The tile is rarely the whole bill. Plan for:

- **Wastage.** Diagonal laying, rooms with many corners and cut pieces mean you order more than the floor area, often by a margin your mason can estimate.
- **Fixing material.** Adhesive or mortar, grout and sealer are usually separate lines.
- **Labour.** Rates differ by city, pattern and tile size. Large formats and diagonal layouts take longer.
- **Transport and unloading.** Heavy stone and fragile clay both need careful handling, and distance changes the charge.
- **Preparation.** An uneven base needs levelling before any tile goes down.

Ask each supplier to list these separately so you are comparing like with like.

## How do you compare two tile quotes fairly?

Put both quotes into one table. Write the unit, the tile size and thickness, the finish, the quantity, what is included (delivery, breakage allowance, fixing material), the date and how long the rate is held. A cheaper rate per piece can hide a smaller tile, so convert both to cost per square foot of covered area. Then check whether wastage was added. If one quote is far below the others, ask what is different instead of assuming a bargain. Always compare the sample you hold with the sample the supplier shows you, since the delivered batch should match.

## What does a tile cost for a living room versus outdoors?

A living room usually uses a smoother, more refined finish and benefits from consistent shade, which can add to the rate. Outdoor areas need grip and resistance to sun, rain and temperature swings, so thicker and rougher surfaces are common. Neither is automatically cheaper. A plain terracotta tile can suit both indoors and outdoors, while a polished tile may be wrong outside. Choose by the job first, then compare rates for tiles that actually suit it. ## How can you cut your tile cost without cutting quality?

Measure the area carefully and order everything in one batch so shade stays even. Choose a size your mason handles easily, since awkward formats raise labour. Use a feature tile in one zone and a plainer one elsewhere. Avoid rush orders, because delivery and urgent labour add cost. Ask for a sample and look at it wet and dry before ordering. Spending a little time on the sample usually costs less than replacing a floor that looks wrong.
