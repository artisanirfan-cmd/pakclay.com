## How do you use pattern tiles in a Pakistani home without it looking busy?

Walk into an older haveli in Lahore and you will notice that the floor is doing half the decorating. Small shapes, repeated colours and a clear layout turn a plain verandah into something people remember. If you are looking at pattern tiles in Pakistan today, the good news is that you do not need a loud printed tile to get that effect. A pattern can come from the tile itself, from the way the pieces are laid, or from mixing two or three colours in a planned order. This guide explains the main routes, where each one suits a room, and what to check before you order.

---

## What are pattern tiles?

**Pattern tiles are tiles whose design comes from a printed or moulded motif, from an unusual shape such as a hexagon or diamond, or from a layout such as herringbone or checkerboard.** The same plain tile can look calm in a straight grid and lively in a diagonal or herringbone layout, so the pattern is often a decision made on site rather than at the shop.

That is useful to know on a budget. A simple rectangular tile laid in a clever pattern can cost no more in material than the same tile laid in rows, although fixers may charge more for complex cuts, so ask first.

## Which pattern tile types can you choose from?

Think of three families, and note that most homes combine at most two of them.

| Family | How the pattern appears | Where it tends to suit |
|---|---|---|
| Shape-led tiles (hexagon, diamond, octagon, half-hexagon) | The outline itself forms the repeat | Verandahs, borders, feature walls |
| Colour-mixed tiles | Several colours in one batch or one layout | Courtyards, ramps, garden paths |
| Layout-led patterns (herringbone, diagonal, running bond) | Plain rectangles or squares laid in a deliberate order | Living rooms, kitchens, entrance halls |

## Are geometric tiles a good choice for floors?

Geometric shapes can work well on floors because the eye reads the repeat and forgives small differences in shade. A hexagon or diamond tends to hide the odd uneven joint better than a plain large square does. The trade-off is more cutting at the edges of a room, where partial pieces are needed, so order extra to cover that waste. Ask your fixer for an estimate of the extra percentage once they have seen the layout.

For outdoor floors, remember that concrete otti pieces and fired clay behave differently. Concrete tiles generally give uniform colour, while fired terracotta shows natural variation from piece to piece. ## How can terracotta rectangles make a pattern?

Terracotta is a good example of a layout-led pattern. A slim rectangle such as the [9x3 inch terracotta tile](/products/9x3-inch-terracotta-tiles) can be laid in a running bond, like bricks, or in a herringbone, and the warm clay colour does the rest. Square tiles such as the [8x8 inch natural terracotta tile](/products/8x8-inch-natural-terracotta-tiles) can go straight or on the diagonal, which makes a small room feel wider because the lines run corner to corner.

Fired clay is porous, so the usual care applies: lay it on a sound base, keep standing water off it and ask about a breathable sealer. Shade can vary between pieces, which many people like in a pattern because it adds depth. Order from one batch so the variation stays within a range you have already seen on a sample.

## Which patterns suit which room?

These are design tendencies, not rules, so trust your own sample board more than any list.

- **Living room or entrance hall:** Diagonal squares or a herringbone of rectangles give movement without extra colour.
- **Kitchen wall:** Small ceramic mosaic pieces in one or two colours can frame a hob or sink area. Pak Clay lists, for example, a [green and white hexagon ceramic mosaic tile](/products/green-and-white-hexagon-ceramic-mosaic-tiles).
- **Verandah or courtyard:** Shape-led otti pieces in two or three colours make a border and a centre field.
- **Staircase or ramp:** Choose a textured, grip-friendly piece and keep the pattern simple, as busy patterns can hide step edges.
- **Feature wall:** One wall of pattern, with plain walls around it, is easier to live with than pattern everywhere.

## How do you keep a pattern from looking messy?

A few habits help. Limit yourself to two or three colours. Keep one plain, calm tile as the base and use the pattern for a border or a central panel. Decide the pattern centre before the first tile goes down, so cuts land symmetrically at both ends of the room. Lay out a dry run of a square metre on the floor and look at it in daylight and at night, because lamp light changes how colours read.

Grout colour matters too. A grout close to the tile colour softens the pattern, while a contrasting grout draws every line. Test both on your sample before the fixer starts.

## What should you check before ordering pattern tiles?

Pattern work leaves little room for surprises, so use this short checklist:

1. Handle a physical sample, not only a photo, and look at it in the light of the room where it will go.
2. Confirm the exact size and thickness so mixed tiles sit level together.
3. Ask how many extra pieces to order for cuts and breakage, based on your layout.
4. Check the finish suits the place: textured for outdoors and wet areas, smoother for indoor walls.
5. Keep the batch number or order together so colours match across deliveries.
6. Get the quote in writing with the date, quantity and delivery location.
