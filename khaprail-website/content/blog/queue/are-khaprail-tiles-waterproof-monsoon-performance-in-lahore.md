## Are khaprail tiles waterproof when the Lahore monsoon arrives?

Every July, Lahore homeowners with a khaprail roof look up at the sky and ask the same thing: will it hold? The honest answer has two parts. The tile itself is not what stops the water, and a well-built khaprail roof can still shed monsoon rain very effectively. This guide explains how a clay tile roof actually keeps rain out, where leaks usually start, and what to ask your contractor and your supplier before the next monsoon.

---

## Are khaprail tiles waterproof?

**Khaprail tiles are fired clay and are not a sealed, waterproof membrane. A khaprail roof works by shedding water: overlapping tiles on a proper slope guide rain downhill and off the roof before it can soak through.** Clay is porous to some degree, and a tile can absorb a little water, so a roof relies on shape, overlap, slope and the layers beneath, not on the tile being watertight like plastic.

We do not publish water-absorption figures for our tiles, so none appear here. If you need a number for a specification, ask for it in writing and for the test it comes from.

## How does a clay tile roof shed rain?

Three things work together:

1. **Slope.** Water runs faster off a steeper roof. A roof that is too flat lets water linger and creep sideways under tiles. Your engineer or experienced mason sets the minimum slope for the profile used.
2. **Overlap.** Each tile sits over the one below, so water moves from tile to tile instead of reaching the roof structure. Correct overlap matters more than the shape name.
3. **Layers below.** Many roofs use an underlay or a sealed deck beneath the tiles as a second line of defence. Ask your contractor what lies under the tiles in your design, because that layer is often what stops a rare wind-driven drop from reaching the ceiling.

Our guide on [how khaprail roof tiles are installed](/blog/how-khaprail-roof-tiles-are-installed-slope-battens-and-overlap) covers slope, battens and overlap in more detail.

## What does the Lahore monsoon demand from a roof?

Lahore's monsoon generally brings short, heavy bursts of rain, often with wind, after months of strong sun. In general terms, this puts four demands on a roof:

- **Fast drainage**, so water does not pool at valleys, parapets or gutters.
- **Tight ridges and hips**, where tiles meet and wind-driven rain can enter.
- **Intact tiles**, since a cracked piece lets water through.
- **Clear outlets**, because blocked gutters and downpipes push water back under the tiles.

A good roof in this climate is one that is checked before the rains rather than after the first drip. A few minutes on a dry day, looking for chipped tiles, shifted pieces and debris, is worth more than any claim about the tile.

## Where do khaprail roofs usually leak?

Leaks in clay tile roofs most often start at details, not in the middle of the field. Common weak points include:

- **Ridge and hip lines**, where bedding or fixings have loosened.
- **Valleys**, where two slopes meet and water concentrates.
- **Walls and parapets**, where flashing or sealing has cracked.
- **Chimneys, pipes and skylights**, which cut through the roof.
- **Broken or slipped tiles**, often after a storm, foot traffic or a fallen branch.
- **Blocked gutters**, which hold water against the eaves.

Our article on [roof leakage with khaprail tiles](/blog/roof-leakage-with-khaprail-tiles-causes-and-how-to-fix-them) goes through each cause and what to do.

## What questions should you ask your contractor?

Before the roof goes on, ask what slope the design uses for your profile, what overlap will be laid, what sits under the tiles, how ridges and valleys will be bedded and sealed, and who is responsible if a leak appears in the first monsoon. Get the answers in writing, since a clear plan is the best protection you have.

## What should you check before the monsoon?

A simple pre-monsoon routine:

1. **Walk the roof safely or use a drone or binoculars**, and look for cracked, chipped or slipped tiles.
2. **Clear gutters, valleys and downpipes** of leaves, dust and debris.
3. **Check ridge and hip tiles** for movement or loose bedding.
4. **Look at the ceiling inside** for stains after the first rain.
5. **Replace broken pieces** with matching tiles, ideally from the same batch.

Our [maintenance guide](/blog/khaprail-tile-maintenance-cleaning-repairs-and-moss-control) lists cleaning and repair basics. Do not walk on a tiled roof unless you know how, because tiles can crack and the surface can be slippery.

Both can shed rain well when laid correctly. Clay is a fired natural material with colour through the body, while concrete tiles are a different product with their own characteristics.
