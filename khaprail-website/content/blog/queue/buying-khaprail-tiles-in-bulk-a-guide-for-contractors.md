## What counts as a bulk order for khaprail tiles?

**A bulk order is any quantity large enough that the supplier would quote you a different rate from a single-house purchase, usually because it needs planning for production, packing and transport.** There is no single industry cut-off, and we do not publish a minimum quantity or a bulk discount online. If you need to know whether your quantity qualifies for a bulk rate, ask us on WhatsApp and we will answer in writing.

## How do you work out the quantity for a large roof?

The steps follow the method in our guide on [how to calculate how many khaprail tiles your roof needs](/blog/how-to-calculate-how-many-khaprail-tiles-your-roof-needs). In short:

1. **Measure the sloped roof surface**, not the floor plan. Slope adds area.
2. **Ask for the coverage of the exact profile**, meaning how many pieces cover a given area at the overlap your crew will use.
3. **Add a margin** for cuts, valleys and breakage. Your own site experience is the best guide for the margin.
4. **Count ridge, hip and edge pieces separately.** They are easy to forget, and they hold up the job if they run short.
5. **For several buildings**, list each roof separately so batches can be matched to each one.

Send us the dimensions or drawings and we reply with the coverage for the profile you choose.

## What should a bulk quotation contain?

A quote that is useful for budgeting states:

- **The profile and finish** (natural red or glazed green).
- **The unit**: price per piece or per square foot of roof coverage.
- **What is included**: field tiles, ridge, hip, edge and any special pieces.
- **The date** the rate was confirmed and how long it holds.
- **Delivery terms**: who arranges transport, where it is unloaded, packing and what is done about breakage.
- **Payment terms** and any advance required.
- **Lead time**, if the quantity needs production planning.

We do not publish a rate sheet online, because a number without a unit and a date is easy to misread. Our guide to [what decides khaprail tile prices](/blog/khaprail-tiles-price-in-pakistan-what-decides-the-rate) covers the factors: profile, finish, quantity, season, transport and included pieces.

## How do you keep colour consistent across a big roof?

Clay is a natural material fired in a kiln, so shade can vary between batches. Practical steps:

- **Order the whole roof in one go**, and tell us if the job will be supplied in stages.
- **Ask for a sample from the batch you will receive**, not only a catalogue piece.
- **Mix tiles from several pallets** as they go up, a common site practice, so slight shade differences blend across the roof.
- **Keep spare tiles** from the same batch for future repairs.

## How do you handle delivery and breakage?

Clay tiles are heavy and can chip, so packing and unloading matter. At order stage, agree in writing:

1. How the tiles will be packed and stacked.
2. Who arranges transport and who is responsible during loading.
3. How breakage is counted and what is done about it. Check on delivery, and note any damage on the receipt.
4. Where the pallets will be stored on site, ideally on level ground, protected from vehicles.

Delivery coverage is agreed per order. We do not list standard delivery cities or charges, so confirm yours before you pay.

## What mistakes do contractors make on bulk tile orders?

These come up on many roofing jobs, whatever the supplier:

- **Ordering from the floor plan.** A sloped roof is larger than the rooms under it, so the count comes up short.
- **Forgetting ridge, hip and edge pieces**, which then have to be ordered late and may come from a different batch.
- **Comparing quotes with different units.** One supplier quotes per piece, another per square foot of coverage, and the totals cannot be compared until both are converted.
- **Skipping the sample.** A catalogue photo does not show texture, edge finish or how the colour looks in your client's light.
- **Leaving delivery terms vague.** If packing, unloading and breakage are not written down, a dispute on site is much harder to settle.
- **Not planning storage.** Pallets left on soft ground or in the path of vehicles are more likely to suffer damage before they reach the roof.

A short checklist agreed with the supplier before the order is confirmed avoids most of them.
