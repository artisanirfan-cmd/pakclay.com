## What decides the price of terracotta tiles in Karachi?

**The price of terracotta tiles in Karachi is set mainly by tile size and thickness, shape, finish, quantity ordered, and the cost of moving heavy, fragile tile from the kiln to your site.** Here is how each one plays out.

**Size and thickness.** A larger or thicker tile uses more clay and more firing energy, and it is more likely to break in transit. Pak Clay lists terracotta tiles in sizes such as 6x6, 8x8, 9x12 and 12x12 inches, among others. Compare prices on the same size, never across sizes.

**Shape.** Squares are the simplest to cut and pack. Hexagons, octagons and star shapes take more care to produce and lay, so they are commonly priced differently from plain squares.

**Finish.** Natural, unglazed terracotta is the classic. Sealed, polished or glazed finishes add steps, and the cost of any sealer or treatment during laying is separate from the tile.

**Quantity.** A small order for one room is handled differently from a contractor buying for a full house. Larger quantities can be quoted differently, and a bulk quote should say clearly what the quantity threshold is.

**Transport and handling.** Terracotta is heavy. For a buyer in Karachi, a major share of the final cost can be freight and packing, so ask what is included and who is responsible for breakage.

**Timing.** Production batches and seasonal demand can change availability and rate, which is why a quote should carry a date.

## Should you price per piece or per square foot?

Be careful here, because suppliers use both. A per-piece rate and a per-square-foot rate are not comparable until you convert them, and the conversion depends on the tile size and the grout joint you plan to leave. A 12x12 inch tile covers roughly one square foot before joints, while a 6x6 inch tile covers a quarter of that, so the number of pieces changes quickly. Ask for the unit in writing and, when you can, ask the supplier to work the total from your area.

Also add a margin for breakage and cutting. Many fitters suggest ordering extra, and your fitter is the right person to set the exact percentage for your layout.

## Does Karachi's climate change what you should buy?

It can. Karachi's humid, salty air is gentler on fired clay than on many metals, but the use of the tile matters. For a covered veranda or indoor room, a natural terracotta tile is a common choice. For an open terrace that gets full sun and the occasional heavy shower, ask about a sealed finish and about a base that slopes enough to drain. Those choices change the total budget, so settle them before you compare quotes. Also tell the supplier whether the floor will see heavy foot traffic, a car or furniture, because that affects which thickness and size are sensible. A supplier who asks you these questions before quoting is usually a good sign.

## Is the cheapest terracotta tile the best value?

Not necessarily. A low rate can come from thinner tile, uneven firing or a mixed batch. Uneven firing tends to show up as variable colour and tiles that chip at the edges. Ask to handle a sample, tap it and listen for a clear ring, and check that several tiles lie flat against each other. A little extra spent on consistent tile often saves more in fitting time and replacements than it costs.

Also remember that fitting, sealing and cleaning are part of the real cost of a terracotta floor. Ask your fitter for a combined estimate.

## Common price mistakes to avoid

- **Comparing different sizes.** Always compare like for like.
- **Mixing units.** Convert per piece and per square foot to the same basis.
- **Trusting undated rates.** A figure from last year is not a quote.
- **Forgetting freight.** For heavy tile, transport can be a large part of the total.
- **Not asking about breakage.** Agree the policy before you pay.
