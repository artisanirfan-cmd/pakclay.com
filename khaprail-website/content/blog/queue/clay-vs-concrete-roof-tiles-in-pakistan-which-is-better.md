## Clay or concrete roof tiles: which should a Pakistani homeowner pick?

If you are planning a pitched roof in Lahore, Islamabad or anywhere in Punjab, you will hear two opinions quickly: "clay lasts forever" and "concrete is cheaper and stronger". Both contain some truth, and neither is the full answer. This guide compares clay roof tiles (khaprail) and concrete roof tiles for Pakistani conditions: monsoon rain, strong sun, dust and the occasional cold snap in the north.

## Are clay roof tiles better than concrete roof tiles in Pakistan?

**Neither material is better in every case. Clay tiles generally keep their colour and suit hot, traditional looks; concrete tiles are often cheaper per piece but heavier and may fade or wear at the surface over time.** The right choice depends on your budget, roof structure, the look you want and who is laying the roof. Rather than trusting a blanket claim, compare samples, weight per square foot and a written quote for both.

## What is the difference in how they are made?

Khaprail is the local name for fired clay roofing tile. Natural clay is shaped and baked in a kiln until it hardens, and the colour comes from the clay itself (natural red) or from a glaze (such as green). Concrete tiles are made from cement, sand and water, moulded and cured, then usually coloured with pigment or a surface coat.

That difference explains most of what follows. Clay colour runs through the body of a natural tile, so chips tend to show the same tone underneath. A coated concrete tile can show a different colour under the coating once the surface wears.

## How do weight and roof structure compare?

Concrete tiles are commonly heavier than clay tiles of a similar profile, though weights vary a lot by profile and maker, so we do not quote a figure here. Weight matters because your roof frame, beams and walls have to carry it, together with rain and wind loads. Ask your structural engineer or contractor to check the frame for the specific tile you intend to use, before you order. This is a design decision, not something a website can settle for you.

## How do they handle Pakistani heat and monsoon rain?

**Heat.** Both materials are mineral and absorb heat slowly compared with thin metal. Neither replaces proper insulation and ventilation under the tile. Our guide to [roof tiles for heat in Pakistan](/blog/best-roof-tiles-for-heat-in-pakistan-keeping-homes-cooler) explains what actually influences room temperature.

**Rain.** Water mostly runs off the tile surface, so correct slope, overlap and underlay matter more than the tile material. Clay is porous, and glazed khaprail sheds water from the surface; concrete also absorbs some water. Poor laying causes more leaks than either material.

**Colour over time.** Natural red clay tends to age quietly. Surface-coloured tiles of any type can fade or weather differently. Ask to see a tile that has been outdoors, not only a new one.

## Which is cheaper: clay or concrete tiles?

Per piece, concrete is often quoted lower, but the full roof cost includes the number of tiles you need, the roof frame strength, transport, breakage, laying labour and later maintenance. A cheaper tile that needs a heavier frame may not save money. Because rates change with season, quantity and distance, we do not publish a fixed price. Ask each supplier for a written quote with the unit and date, and compare like for like. ## Quick comparison

| Factor | Clay (khaprail) | Concrete |
|---|---|---|
| Raw material | Fired natural clay | Cement, sand, pigment |
| Colour | Natural red or glazed | Pigment or surface coat |
| Weight | Usually lighter | Usually heavier |
| Look | Traditional, warm | Wide range of profiles |
| Upfront rate | Varies by profile | Often quoted lower per piece |
| Breakage in transport | Brittle, pack with care | Also breakable |

These are general tendencies, not guarantees. Individual products differ.

## What should you check before choosing?

1. **Roof frame capacity.** Ask your engineer to confirm the frame for the tile weight.
2. **Slope and overlap.** Confirm the minimum pitch for the profile.
3. **Sample outdoors.** Leave samples in sun and wet for a few weeks.
4. **Tap test.** A clear ring from clay usually points to an even firing.
5. **Batch consistency.** Order the whole roof together so colour matches.
6. **Spares.** Keep extra pieces for future repairs.
7. **Written quote.** Dated, with the unit stated.

## When does clay make more sense?

Clay is a natural fit if you want a traditional or Mediterranean look, a colour that comes from the material, or a roof for a house where appearance is part of the value. It also pairs well with terracotta floors, jali and gutka for a consistent look across the property. Browse the [categories](/categories) to see the matching products.

## When might concrete make more sense?

If your budget is tight, your frame is already designed for a heavier tile, or you want a specific profile or colour that a supplier offers, concrete can be a reasonable option. Choose a reputable maker and ask for references you can visit.

## How long do roof tiles last, and what upkeep do they need?

Lifespan claims for both materials vary widely online, and we do not publish a year figure for our tiles, because it depends on firing quality, laying, climate and upkeep. What you can control is maintenance. Clear gutters and valleys before the monsoon so water does not sit, replace cracked pieces promptly, and have a contractor check ridge and hip pieces every few years. Moss and dust can build up on shaded slopes of any tile type, and a gentle clean is usually enough. Keep spare tiles from the original batch, because colour matches best within one batch and replacements bought years later may differ slightly. Ask every supplier what upkeep they advise and what they will put in writing about replacements.

## The bottom line

Clay and concrete both work on Pakistani roofs when they are laid correctly. Decide using weight, frame strength, look, a dated quote and a sample you have actually seen outdoors. The laying quality will matter as much as the material.
