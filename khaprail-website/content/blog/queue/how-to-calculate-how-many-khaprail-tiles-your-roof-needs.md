## How many khaprail tiles do you need per square foot of roof?

"How many khaprail tiles per square foot?" is one of the first questions a Lahore homeowner asks, and the honest answer is that it depends on the tile. A large tile with a generous overlap covers more roof per piece than a small tile with a tight overlap, so there is no single number that fits every khaprail profile. What you can do is learn the method, measure your roof and one sample tile, and work out your own figure in a few minutes. This guide shows how, with a clearly labelled example, so you can check any quote you receive.

---

## What are khaprail tiles?

**Khaprail tiles are fired clay roofing tiles, baked in a kiln from natural clay. They are laid in overlapping rows on a sloped roof, so each tile covers only part of its own surface; the rest is hidden under the row above.** That hidden part is the reason tile counts are not simply roof area divided by tile area.

## What is the basic formula for counting khaprail tiles?

The method has three steps:

1. **Measure the roof area** that will be tiled, in square feet.
2. **Find the coverage of one tile**, meaning the part of one tile that stays visible once it is laid with the overlap you plan to use.
3. **Divide the roof area by the coverage of one tile**, then add an allowance for cuts and breakage.

In short: tiles needed = roof area ÷ coverage per tile, plus an allowance.

## How do you work out the coverage of one tile?

Coverage per tile is the exposed length multiplied by the exposed width, not the full size of the tile. To find it:

- Take a sample tile and lay a second one on the first, in the overlapped position your contractor intends to use. Measure how much of the lower tile remains visible along the slope. That is the **exposed length**, also called the gauge.
- Measure the **exposed width** across the roof. Interlocking or side-lapped profiles lose a little width to the side overlap, and flat tiles laid edge to edge lose almost none.
- Multiply the two. Convert to square feet by dividing square inches by 144.

Ask your supplier for the recommended gauge and side lap for your exact profile. Do not borrow figures from a different tile or from a foreign website, because profiles differ and a small change in overlap changes the count noticeably.

## Can you see a worked example?

The numbers below are **illustrative only**. They are not the measurements of any Pak Clay tile.

- Roof area to be tiled: 1,000 sq ft.
- Suppose a sample tile shows an exposed length of 10 inches and an exposed width of 8 inches. Coverage = 80 square inches, which is about 0.56 sq ft.
- Tiles needed = 1,000 ÷ 0.56, which is about 1,800 tiles.
- Add an allowance for breakage and cuts. Many roofers allow roughly 5 to 10 percent, but ask your contractor for the figure suited to your roof shape. A 10 percent allowance here gives about 1,980 tiles.

Swap in your own measurements and you have a working estimate. Tile counts are also sold in different ways, for example per piece, per dozen or per square foot, so check the unit on every quote.

## How do you measure the roof area correctly?

Many people measure the floor plan and stop there, but a sloped roof has more surface than the plan underneath it. Measure the actual surface along the slope, or ask your contractor to calculate it from the pitch. A simple approach:

- Split the roof into rectangles and triangles, measure each along the slope and add them.
- For hip roofs, measure each plane separately.
- Subtract openings such as skylights and chimneys, but add a little back for the cuts around them.

A steeper roof has a larger surface for the same footprint, so two houses with the same floor area can need different tile counts. Our guide on [how khaprail roof tiles are installed](/blog/how-khaprail-roof-tiles-are-installed-slope-battens-and-overlap) explains how slope and overlap are linked.

## What else do you need besides the plain tiles?

A tile count for the main roof is only part of the order. Ask your contractor to list:

- **Ridge and hip pieces**, counted by length rather than area.
- **Verge, eave and valley details** where special pieces or cut tiles are needed.
- **Fixings, battens and underlay**, which are priced separately from the tiles.
- **Spare tiles** for future repairs. Colour can vary slightly between batches, so spares from the same order match best.

## How much extra should you order?

An allowance of about 5 to 10 percent is commonly used, and roofs with many hips, valleys or cut edges may need more. Order the full quantity in one go, from one batch, where you can. If you have to top up later, the colour and size may differ a little. Whatever allowance you choose, write it into the quote so it is clear what is being supplied.
