## What are tuff tiles, and are they the right paving for your Pakistani driveway?

Ask for "tuff tiles" in Lahore, Rawalpindi or Faisalabad and you will usually be shown the same thing: thick, interlocking paving pieces laid on driveways, parking bays, footpaths and garden paths. The name is a trade label, not a standard, and it is often typed as "tough tiles" or "tuff tile" in search. That looseness is the problem. Two shops can quote the same word for pieces of very different thickness and quality. This guide explains what the term usually means, how interlocking paver tiles behave, what the alternatives are, and what to ask before you order.

---

## What are tuff tiles?

**Tuff tiles are thick, interlocking paving pieces, typically moulded from concrete, used for outdoor surfaces that carry traffic, such as driveways, parking areas and footpaths.** In Pakistan the word is a market name rather than a technical grade. It usually points to a heavy pavement block that locks with its neighbours, as opposed to a thin ceramic floor tile.

Because there is no single definition, always ask the supplier for the material, the exact size and the thickness of the piece in your hand. A published brochure photo tells you very little.

## How do interlocking paver tiles work?

Interlocking pavers are laid close together over a compacted base and a levelled bedding layer, with the joints filled with fine sand. Because each piece presses against its neighbours, a load on one piece is shared across the surface rather than resting on a single tile. That is why pavers tend to cope with parked cars better than a large thin tile glued to a slab.

Two other practical points. First, a paver surface drains through its joints and across its fall, so water does not need a sealed membrane. Second, individual pieces can be lifted and re-laid if a pipe or cable needs repair, which a poured surface does not allow.

## Tuff tiles or terracotta and clay: what is the difference?

The look and the behaviour are different. A concrete paver usually reads grey or coloured with a smooth, even finish. A clay paver or terracotta piece has a warmer, earthy colour that comes from the fired clay. Clay tends to stay a little cooler in strong sun than dark concrete, though thickness and colour play a part, and it varies from piece to piece in shade.

On strength, do not assume one is automatically better. A thick, well-made concrete paver and a thick, well-fired clay paver can both serve a driveway, while a thin version of either can fail. ## Where do interlocking pavers make sense?

Typical uses include:

- **Driveways and car parking.** The locking pattern spreads wheel loads when the base is sound.
- **Footpaths and garden paths.** Small pieces follow curves and borders easily.
- **Commercial frontage and yards.** Hard-wearing surfaces that can be repaired in sections.
- **Roadside and boundary strips.** Where water should be able to drain away.

They are a poor match for indoor rooms and for polished, formal entrances where a flat, fine surface is wanted. For porches, see our [porch tiles guide](/blog/porch-tiles-in-pakistan-designs-that-handle-sun-and-rain).

## What matters more than the tile: the base?

A paver surface is only as good as what lies underneath. A typical approach is to excavate, compact the soil, add and compact a sub-base, spread a thin levelled bedding layer, lay the pieces, then compact them and brush sand into the joints. A firm edge around the outside stops the outer pieces from creeping. Depth and materials depend on soil type and load, so ask whoever is laying your pavers to describe their base before work begins. Plan a gentle fall so rain moves away from the house.

Most complaints about sinking or rocking pavers come from a weak or uneven base, not from the paver itself.

## What should you check before ordering?

- **Get the material and thickness in writing,** not just the trade name.
- **Describe the load.** Cars only, or heavier vehicles too?
- **Ask for a physical sample,** and test its grip with a wet shoe.
- **Check colour in your own sun,** since shade changes through the day.
- **Plan base and drainage** before the pavers arrive.
- **Order a margin** for cuts and later repairs, all from one batch.
