## What are khaprail tiles and how do they work?

**Khaprail tiles are fired clay roof tiles, shaped from natural clay and baked in a kiln. They are laid in overlapping rows on a sloped roof so that rain runs from tile to tile and off the roof.**

### 1. What does "khaprail" mean?
It is the common local name for clay roof tiles in Pakistan. Today it covers curved, flat and glazed clay roofing tiles.

### 2. Are khaprail tiles the same as terracotta tiles?
Both are fired clay. Khaprail usually means roof tiles, while terracotta tile usually means floor and wall tiles, though the material is related. See our [types of tiles guide](/blog/different-types-of-tiles-explained-clay-ceramic-stone-and-cement).

### 3. What colours are available?
Pak Clay lists natural red and glazed green. Compare real samples in your own light, since screens change colour. Our [colours guide](/blog/khaprail-tiles-colors-natural-red-glazed-green-and-more) explains the finishes.

### 4. What profiles are there?
Spanish, French and flat profiles are listed, including flat half-circle and flat rectangular shapes. The [profiles guide](/blog/spanish-french-and-flat-khaprail-tiles-which-profile-suits-your-roof) compares them.

### 5. Is khaprail the same as concrete roof tile?
No. Clay is fired in a kiln, concrete is cast and cured. Each has trade-offs; see [clay vs concrete roof tiles](/blog/clay-vs-concrete-roof-tiles-in-pakistan-which-is-better).

## Do khaprail roofs work in Pakistan's climate?

### 6. Are khaprail tiles waterproof?
Not like a sealed membrane. A khaprail roof sheds rain through slope, overlap and detailing, often with an underlay. Read more in our [monsoon guide](/blog/are-khaprail-tiles-waterproof-monsoon-performance-in-lahore).

### 7. Do khaprail roofs keep a house cooler?
Clay is often chosen for hot climates and a ventilated tile roof can help, but results depend on the design, insulation and colour. See [roof tiles for heat](/blog/best-roof-tiles-for-heat-in-pakistan-keeping-homes-cooler).

### 8. Do they suit humid or coastal cities like Karachi?
Many people use them there, and glazed finishes are often considered for easier cleaning. Read our [Karachi guide](/blog/best-khaprail-tiles-in-karachi-humidity-heat-and-what-lasts).

### 9. What about cold, rainy places like Islamabad?
Ask your supplier for any frost or freeze information in writing for the exact tile. We do not make cold-weather claims without a spec. See the [Islamabad guide](/blog/best-khaprail-tiles-in-islamabad-a-buyers-guide-for-rain-and-cold).

### 10. Will wind lift the tiles?
Tiles are usually fixed or interlocked according to the design and local conditions. Your contractor and engineer decide fixing, so raise wind with them.

## How are khaprail tiles installed and looked after?

### 11. What slope does a khaprail roof need?
It depends on the profile and the design. Confirm the minimum slope for your tile with your supplier and engineer. Our [installation guide](/blog/how-khaprail-roof-tiles-are-installed-slope-battens-and-overlap) explains the principle.

### 12. Can I lay them on an existing flat concrete roof?
That needs a structural check, a frame and proper detailing. Ask an engineer before you buy.

### 13. How many tiles do I need?
It depends on the tile's coverage and your roof area, plus an allowance for cuts and breakage. Use our [tile calculation guide](/blog/how-to-calculate-how-many-khaprail-tiles-your-roof-needs).

### 14. How do I maintain a khaprail roof?
Inspect it twice a year, clear gutters, brush off moss gently and replace cracked tiles. See [maintenance and moss control](/blog/khaprail-tile-maintenance-cleaning-repairs-and-moss-control).

### 15. Why does my khaprail roof leak?
Leaks usually start at ridges, valleys, flashings, cracked tiles or poor drainage rather than the plain tile surface. See [causes and fixes](/blog/roof-leakage-with-khaprail-tiles-causes-and-how-to-fix-them).

### 16. How long do they last?
Fired clay is durable and many clay roofs last for decades, but life depends on the product, installation and upkeep. We do not promise a number; ask for any warranty in writing.
