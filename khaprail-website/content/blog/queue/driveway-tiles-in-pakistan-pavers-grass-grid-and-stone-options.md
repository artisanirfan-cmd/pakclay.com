## Which driveway tiles actually last in Pakistan's heat, rain and traffic?

A driveway in Lahore does more work than most homeowners expect. In summer the surface bakes under direct sun, in monsoon it takes sheets of water, and every day it carries cars, rickshaws and the occasional delivery truck. If you are comparing driveway tiles in Pakistan, the real question is not which tile looks best in a photo, but which one stays level, drains and does not crack under load. This guide covers the main options, what each is typically good at, and what to ask before you order.

---

## What are driveway tiles?

**Driveway tiles are paving pieces made to carry vehicle weight as well as foot traffic, usually laid as interlocking pavers, open grass-grid units or thicker stone and clay pieces over a compacted base.** They are different from indoor floor tiles. A thin, glossy tile suited to a bedroom will often chip, slip or crack when a car parks on it.

Two things decide how a driveway behaves: the piece itself (material, thickness, shape) and the base underneath it. Many failed driveways have a perfectly good tile on a poor base.

## Which materials are used for driveways?

Here is a general comparison. These are typical tendencies, not test results, so always check a physical sample and ask your supplier about suitability for your load.

| Option | Typical strength | Typical drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Concrete or clay pavers | Interlock tightly, can be lifted and re-laid for repairs | Need a well-compacted base and edge restraint |
| Grass-grid pavers | Water drains through, softer green look | Not ideal where tyres turn sharply every day |
| Natural stone | Rugged, natural surface with good grip | Heavy, and shade varies piece to piece |
| Large thin tiles | Clean look | Usually unsuitable for vehicles unless made for it |

## Are pavers better than plain tiles for a driveway?

In many cases, yes. Pavers are generally thicker and are designed to be laid in a pattern that locks pieces together, which spreads the weight of a vehicle across the surface instead of loading one tile. Small formats can also follow curves and gates more easily. The trade-off is that pavers depend on the bedding: if the base moves, the surface can sink or rock. A single oversized thin tile has no such locking and is more likely to crack under a wheel.

## Do grass-grid pavers work for car parking?

Grass-grid pavers are open-cell units that let grass or gravel sit in the openings while the grid carries the load. They are commonly chosen for parking bays, secondary driveways and garden access because rainwater can soak in instead of running onto the road. They tend to work best where cars park and move slowly rather than turn tightly in the same spot every day. Grass also needs sun and some care, so a fully shaded or heavily used lane may be better with solid pavers. Tell your supplier how often the area is used before choosing.

## How should a driveway base be prepared?

This is the step most people skip. A typical approach is to excavate, compact the soil, lay and compact a sub-base, then add a levelled bedding layer before the pavers go on, with a firm edge so the outer pieces do not creep. The depth and material depend on your soil and load, so ask the person laying it to describe the base before work begins. Also plan the fall: a gentle slope away from the house and towards a drain stops water collecting at the gate or garage.

## What about heat, grip and colour?

Dark, dense surfaces tend to hold more heat in direct sun, while lighter or more porous ones usually stay a little cooler. A textured face tends to grip better when wet than a polished one, which matters on a sloped driveway during rain. Natural earthy tones also hide dust and tyre marks better than very light colours. Take a sample outside at midday, put your hand on it, then wet it and press a wet shoe on it. That test tells you more than a brochure.

## Driveway tiles vs other outdoor floors: what is different?

A porch or patio mainly sees footsteps, so grip and look come first. A driveway adds weight and turning forces, so thickness, locking pattern and base come first. If you are covering both, you may use different pieces for each: a finer terracotta or paver for the porch and a thicker paver or grid for the vehicle area. ## What should you check before ordering?

- **Describe the load.** Cars only, or also heavier vehicles? Say so when you ask for a quote.
- **Ask for the thickness** of the actual piece, not only a list.
- **Plan the base and drainage** before the pavers arrive.
- **Test a wet sample** for grip.
- **Order a margin** for cuts and later repairs, and buy everything together because natural materials vary by batch.
- **Get the quote in writing,** with unit, inclusions and date.
