## What are terracotta floor tiles?

**Terracotta floor tiles are fired clay tiles in natural red or orange tones, made by shaping clay and baking it in a kiln.** The name means baked earth. The colour runs through the body of the tile, so scratches and edge chips do not reveal a different colour underneath. Shade and surface vary from tile to tile, which gives a floor its handmade character.

## Do terracotta floors suit Islamabad's weather?

Islamabad has warm summers, a monsoon with heavy rain and cool, sometimes cold, winters. In general terms:

- **Indoors**, terracotta is a comfortable, warm-looking floor that many people like in living rooms, kitchens, dining areas and corridors. Clay can feel cool underfoot on a winter morning, so some households add rugs or consider heating, which is a design choice to plan early.
- **Outdoors**, terracotta is a traditional choice for verandas, terraces and garden paths. The main issues are water and cold. Wet clay can be slippery, and water trapped in a porous surface through cold nights is a concern for any masonry. We do not publish frost, water-absorption or slip ratings for our tiles, so ask for a physical sample and discuss your site with your contractor.

## How do you choose a tile for an indoor floor?

For a living room, kitchen or corridor, think in this order:

1. **Room size and light.** Larger pieces such as 12x12 suit open rooms with fewer joints, while 6x6 or 4x4 suits smaller spaces and curved edges.
2. **Layout.** Rectangles can be laid in running bond or herringbone for a more designed look.
3. **Maintenance.** Natural terracotta is porous in general, so most installers seal or wax it after laying. Ask your installer which sealer they recommend.
4. **Shade variation.** Mix tiles from several boxes while laying so differences spread evenly.

## How do you choose a tile for an outdoor floor in Islamabad?

For verandas, terraces and paths:

- **Plan drainage first.** Give the floor a slight fall so water runs off instead of sitting.
- **Choose texture with care.** A matte, natural surface is usually less slippery when wet than a polished one, but we make no slip-rating claim, so test a wet sample.
- **Ask about the base.** A proper, well-compacted, dry base matters more outdoors than any tile choice.
- **Think about the cold season.** Discuss the winter conditions of your plot with your contractor, especially if it is exposed or higher up.

## Are terracotta tiles better than ceramic or stone?

Not in general; they are different. Terracotta has a warm, natural, slightly irregular look. Ceramic tiles are typically more uniform and can be glazed for easy cleaning. Stone has its own weight, cost and cold feel. Choose by room, budget and the look you like.
