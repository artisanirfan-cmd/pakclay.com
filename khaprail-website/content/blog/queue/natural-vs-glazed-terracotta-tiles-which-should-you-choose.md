## Natural or glazed terracotta tiles: which one belongs in your Pakistani home?

A homeowner in Lahore standing in a tile showroom usually faces the same fork: the warm, matte, slightly uneven natural terracotta that looks like old courtyards, or a glazed tile with a smooth, coloured, wipe-clean face. Both are fired clay, and both have a place. The right choice depends on the room, the weather it faces, how much cleaning you want to do and the look you are after. This guide compares natural vs glazed terracotta tiles in plain terms, with the trade-offs on each side, so you can walk into a showroom with the right questions.

---

## What is the difference between natural and glazed terracotta?

**Natural terracotta is fired clay left unglazed, so you see and touch the clay itself, with its warm colour running through the tile. Glazed tile has a glass-like coating fired onto the surface, which adds colour and a smoother, less absorbent face.**

Underneath, the body of the tile is similar. The difference is the surface, and the surface decides how the tile looks, how it handles water and stains, how it feels underfoot and how it is cared for.

## How do they look and feel?

Natural terracotta has an earthy, matte look. Shades vary from piece to piece, from pale orange to deeper red, and small differences in size and texture are part of the appeal. It suits courtyards, verandas, rustic kitchens and heritage-style rooms.

Glazed tiles can be glossy, satin or matte, and can come in colours that clay never takes on its own, such as green, blue or white. They look cleaner and more uniform, which suits modern kitchens and bathrooms. Pak Clay's catalogue includes glazed ceramic mosaics for walls alongside natural terracotta, and it helps to look at both in your own light.

## Which is easier to clean?

Glazed tile generally wins. Its coated surface is far less porous, so spills sit on top rather than soaking in, and a damp cloth is often enough. Natural terracotta is porous, so oil, tea, turmeric and hard water can leave marks if the surface is not protected.

Natural tiles are usually sealed after installation to reduce staining, and sealing may need renewing over time. Ask your supplier or tiler what product suits your tile and how often to reapply it. Skipping this step is the most common reason people feel disappointed with terracotta in kitchens.

## Which is better for floors, walls, bathrooms and outdoors?

There is no single answer, but these general patterns hold:

| Place | Natural terracotta | Glazed tile |
|---|---|---|
| Living room floor | Warm, traditional look; seal it | Uniform, easy to wipe |
| Kitchen wall or backsplash | Possible with sealing; stains more easily | Often more practical near the hob and sink |
| Bathroom wall | Works if sealed and detailed properly | Usually simpler to keep clean |
| Courtyard, veranda, porch | Common choice; texture can help grip | Check slip resistance, since glossy glaze can be slippery when wet |
| Roof | Natural red is common and stays colour-through | Glazed khaprail gives colour and a sleeker finish |

Treat the table as a starting point. Slip resistance, water absorption and suitability for outdoor use differ by product, so ask the supplier to confirm them for the exact tile in writing instead of assuming from the finish name. ## Do natural and glazed tiles age differently?

Yes. Natural terracotta tends to develop a patina as it is walked on, cleaned and sealed, and many people enjoy that lived-in look. Stains can become part of that character or can be a nuisance, depending on your taste.

Glazed tile keeps its colour on the surface and tends to look the same for longer, but a hard impact can chip the glaze and expose the clay body beneath, which then shows as a different colour. Both finishes benefit from keeping a few spare tiles from your original batch, since colour can vary slightly between batches.

## What about cost?

We do not quote a price here, because there is no single rate for either finish. Price depends on tile size and thickness, shape, finish, how the batch was made, quantity, transport and the date you ask. Glazing adds a production step, so it can raise the cost, but a plain natural tile with a good sealing job also carries a cost, and both depend on the specific product. The only reliable way to compare is to request a dated written quote for each shortlisted tile that states the unit, whether per piece or per square foot. ## How should you choose between them?

Run through these questions in order:

1. **Where will it go?** Wet wall, kitchen splash zone, outdoor floor or living area?
2. **How much upkeep will you accept?** If the answer is "almost none", lean glazed. If you are happy to reseal, natural is fine.
3. **What look do you want?** Earthy and varied points to natural; clean and colourful points to glazed.
4. **What does the supplier say about the specific tile?** Ask for sizes, finish, slip and absorption details in writing.
5. **How does the sample look in your light?** Put it on the actual floor or wall, wet it and look at it in the morning and evening.

A free sample is the cheapest way to avoid regret, so ask for both finishes side by side.
