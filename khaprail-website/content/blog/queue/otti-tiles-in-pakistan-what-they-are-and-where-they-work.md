## What are otti tiles?

**Otti tiles are small, irregular or geometrically shaped pieces laid together to form an outdoor floor, usually with narrow joints between them. The result looks like a mosaic or crazy paving, and it is used for paths, porches, ramps, car parking areas and garden surrounds.**

The word is used loosely in the market. In the Pak Clay catalogue, the otti pieces are concrete-based outdoor floor tiles in random, diamond, half-hexagon and octagon shapes, with some in multi-colour mixes and one in a terracotta colour. Other sellers may use the same word for stone or clay pieces, so always ask what the material is.

## Where do otti tiles work well?

Otti is mostly an outdoor, informal floor. Typical uses include garden paths, house entrances, courtyards, parking spaces, ramps and the edge strips around lawns. The many small joints and uneven edges can give a wet foot some grip, though how much depends on the surface texture of the actual piece. Test a wet sample before you commit.

It is less suited to places where you want a smooth, formal finish, such as a living room or a polished veranda. For those, a [terracotta floor tile](/blog/best-terracotta-floor-tiles-in-lahore-sizes-shapes-and-finish) or a paver may look cleaner.

## How is otti different from pavers and terracotta?

Pavers are usually uniform, interlocking pieces; otti is shaped to fit a random or mosaic layout. Terracotta tiles are fired clay and are porous, while concrete otti is cement-based. ## Does otti suit Pakistan's sun and rain?

Outdoor floors in Pakistan face strong summer sun, heavy monsoon bursts and dust. Concrete otti is generally a hard-wearing material, and the narrow joints help water drain through, but the floor is only as good as the base. A compacted base, a slight fall away from the house and proper joint filling matter more than the tile itself. Shaded, damp corners can grow moss over time, so plan for occasional sweeping and washing.

We do not publish durability numbers or temperature figures, because they depend on the batch, the thickness and the laying. Ask for a sample, put it in the sun and see how it feels at midday.

## How do you lay otti tiles properly?

General practice, which your tile fixer should confirm:

1. Mark the area and plan the fall so water runs away from walls.
2. Compact the sub-base and make it level.
3. Lay the pieces on a suitable bed, matching the shapes as you go and keeping joints narrow and even.
4. Fill the joints and clean the surface.
5. Let the floor cure before heavy use, and keep vehicles off it until the fixer says it is ready.

For parking areas and ramps, ask specifically about thickness and bedding for vehicle loads. Do not assume that any piece is suitable for a car.

## What should you check before ordering?

- **Confirm the material.** Concrete, stone and clay can all be called otti.
- **Name the area.** Path, porch, ramp or parking space.
- **Wet-test the sample** for grip, and look at it in daylight.
- **Order one batch** with a margin for cuts, because colours can vary between batches.
- **Agree the base and drainage** with your fixer before the material arrives.
- **Get the quote in writing**, with unit, inclusions and date.

## How do you maintain an otti floor?

Maintenance is light but not zero. Sweep regularly so grit does not wear the surface, wash off oil and mud, and clear moss from shaded corners before it spreads. If a piece cracks or sinks, a local mason can lift and replace it, which is one practical advantage of a floor made of many small units. Keep a few spare pieces from your original batch, because colours and shapes can differ slightly between batches and a later match may not be exact. Avoid harsh acids on any cement-based surface unless your supplier confirms they are safe for your tile.

## Can you mix otti with other tiles?

Often, yes. Many owners frame an otti path with a border of paver or terracotta pieces, or use otti for the driveway and a terracotta tile near the door. Mixing materials works best when the thickness is similar and the edges are planned in advance, so the join does not trip anyone. Draw the layout on paper, show it to your fixer and order samples of each material together so you can judge the colours side by side.
