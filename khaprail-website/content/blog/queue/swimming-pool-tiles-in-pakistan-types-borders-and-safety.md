## Which swimming pool tiles work in Pakistan?

A pool tile has a harder life than a bathroom tile. It sits under water all day, takes sun, chlorine and temperature swings, and people walk on the edges with wet feet. ## What are swimming pool tiles?

**Swimming pool tiles are tiles used to line a pool's walls, floor, steps and surrounding edge, chosen to tolerate constant water, pool chemicals and sun while staying easy to clean.** They are typically small glazed or glass-like tiles for the water zone and a textured, slip-resistant tile for the deck, and Pak Clay lists several swimming pool tile designs and a border tile in the [product catalogue](/products).

## What types of pool tiles are used?

Most pools combine more than one kind of tile.

- **Glazed ceramic tiles.** A glazed surface does not soak up water the way open clay does, which is why pools and bathrooms usually use glazed tiles. Many come as small mosaic squares that follow curves easily.
- **Glass mosaic.** Small glass tiles are common for the water zone and give a bright finish, though they need careful fixing.
- **Porcelain tiles.** Dense porcelain is used for floors and decks where strength matters.
- **Natural stone and clay for decks.** Textured stone or unglazed clay can give grip on the surrounding area, but clay is porous and needs thought about staining and sealing.

Which one suits you depends on budget, pool design and how the pool is built. Ask your pool builder what the pool's structure and waterproofing system recommend.

## What does a pool border tile do?

The border, often called the waterline or band tile, runs along the top of the pool wall where the water meets the edge. It makes cleaning easier because scum and oils tend to collect at the waterline, and a tile surface wipes cleaner than plaster. It also gives the pool a visible outline and often carries the pool's colour accent. Border tiles come in different heights and patterns, and some designs are chosen so the pattern hides small marks. Check the border for thickness and edge finish, because a rough or sharp edge is uncomfortable for swimmers' hands. ## How do you choose a safe, slip-resistant surface?

Safety is the part people forget in the excitement of choosing colours. Steps, ramps and shallow ends, and the deck around the pool, are where wet feet are most likely to slip. Look for a textured or matt surface in those areas rather than a glossy one. Glossy tiles look good on a wall but can be slippery underfoot when wet. Ask the supplier how the tile is rated for wet slip resistance, if it carries a rating, and do not accept a vague claim. Bring a sample, wet it and test it with a bare foot. Also think about heat: in Pakistani summers a dark deck tile can become hot underfoot in direct sun, so lighter shades or shaded areas can be more comfortable.

## Will pool chemicals and weather damage tiles?

Chlorine and other pool chemicals, along with sun and temperature changes, are part of normal pool life. Tiles that are made for wet areas generally cope, but the adhesive, grout and waterproofing behind them are just as important. A tile that is fine can still come loose if the fixing is wrong. Ask your builder which adhesive and grout suit the pool and whether the grout is resistant to pool chemicals. In Pakistan's climate, large temperature swings between day and night and between summer and winter can stress joints, so movement joints and good workmanship matter. Frost is rarely a concern in Lahore but may matter in colder northern areas, so tell your supplier where the pool will be.

## What colours and patterns work for pools?

Blue and turquoise are the obvious choices, since they make the water look lighter. White and pale tiles can make the water look brighter and make dirt easier to spot. Darker tiles can look dramatic but show scale and dust. Mosaic patterns, borders and a contrasting waterline tile can add detail. Remember that water changes how a tile looks, so view the sample wet and in daylight, and ask for a dry-and-wet comparison. Keep patterns simple if you want easier cleaning.

## What should you ask a supplier before ordering?

Keep a short list. Is the tile suitable for permanent immersion, and in what use does the supplier recommend it? What are the size and thickness? Is the surface textured or glossy, and is there a slip rating for the steps and deck? Is the full quantity from one batch, since shade can vary between firings? What adhesive and grout does the supplier suggest? How are broken pieces handled? Get the answers in writing.
