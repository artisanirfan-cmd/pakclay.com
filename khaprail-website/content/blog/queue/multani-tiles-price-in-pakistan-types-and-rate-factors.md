## What decides Multani tiles price in Pakistan?

Search for Multani tiles price in Pakistan and you will find a lot of undated ranges, mixed units and listings for tiles that are not Multani at all. The reason is simple: "Multani" is a trade word, not a fixed product. Two shops can quote very different rates for "Multani tiles" because they mean different things. This guide explains what the term usually covers, what moves the rate, and how to get a quote you can actually compare.

## What are Multani tiles?

**Multani tiles is a general name in Pakistan for traditional clay-based tiles linked to the Multan region, and many people also use it for blue-and-white glazed decorative tiles.** There is no single standard, so the meaning depends on who you ask. Some buyers mean plain fired clay floor tiles in warm earth tones. Others mean hand-painted or glazed tiles in blues and whites, often seen on shrines, mosques and heritage-style walls.

That ambiguity matters for price. A plain unglazed clay square and a decorated glazed tile need different clay preparation, different firing and different finishing, so they cannot share one rate. Before you compare quotes, write down exactly which type you mean.

## Which types of Multani-style tiles are there?

In general terms you will meet three groups:

- **Plain clay (terracotta) tiles.** Unglazed, warm red-brown, used on floors, verandahs and courtyards.
- **Glazed decorative tiles.** A coloured or patterned glaze over a clay or ceramic body, popular for feature walls and entrances.
- **Small-format mosaic or square tiles** in single colours such as sky blue, used for borders and wall panels.

## How much do Multani tiles cost per square foot?

We do not publish a fixed Multani tile rate, because the figure depends on too many variables to print honestly. Any website that shows one number without a date, a size and a unit is giving you a guess. We would rather give a dated written quote for your exact tile.

**What does move the rate?** These factors tend to matter most:

1. **Type and finish.** Plain clay is usually simpler to make than a decorated glazed tile.
2. **Size and thickness.** Larger, thicker tiles use more clay and more kiln space, and break more in transit.
3. **Glaze and decoration.** Colour, pattern and hand work add steps.
4. **Quantity.** Larger, single-batch orders are usually easier to price than a few boxes.
5. **Batch and grade.** Tiles sorted for tighter size and colour consistency can cost more than mixed stock.
6. **Distance and packing.** Heavy, breakable goods need careful packing and freight, which varies by city.
7. **Season.** Kiln firing and drying depend on weather, and demand rises when building picks up.

## How do you compare two Multani tile quotes fairly?

Many price disputes come from mismatched quotes. Check that both state:

- The **exact tile**: size in inches or millimetres, thickness, finish and colour.
- The **unit**: per square foot, per piece or per box, and how many pieces are in a box.
- The **date** the rate was confirmed, and how long it holds.
- What is **included**: packing, loading, delivery, breakage allowance.
- Whether the rate is for a **sample batch or the full order**.

A quote that says "Multani tiles, 120 per piece" is not a quote. Ask for the details above in writing.

## Multani tiles or terracotta: which should you choose?

If you want a warm, earthy floor, plain terracotta or clay is usually what people mean, and it suits verandahs, kitchens and living areas when laid on a sound base. If you want blue-and-white pattern, you are in glazed decorative territory, which often works better on walls than floors because glaze can be slippery when wet. ## Can Multani tiles be used outdoors?

The short answer is sometimes, depending on the tile.

Fired clay is porous, so outdoor use needs planning: a firm sloped base for drainage, a breathable sealer chosen with your fixer, and a surface that is not slick when wet. In places that freeze, check with the maker before choosing any porous tile. Glazed tiles shed water better but can be slippery on a wet outdoor floor, so choose carefully for steps, ramps and porches. ## What should you check before you order?

1. **See a sample.** Colour and glaze vary, and photos mislead.
2. **Compare several pieces** from one batch for size and shade.
3. **Order everything in one go**, plus a margin for cuts and breakage.
4. **Ask about packing and transport** before you pay.
5. **Keep spares** from the same batch for future repairs.
6. **Get the quote in writing**, dated, with the unit stated.
