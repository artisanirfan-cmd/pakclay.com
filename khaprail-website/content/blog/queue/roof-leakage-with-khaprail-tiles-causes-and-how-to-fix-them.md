## Why does a khaprail roof leak, and how do you find the cause?

A wet patch on the ceiling after the first heavy rain is stressful, and the instinct is to blame the tiles. Often the tile is fine and the water has found a gap somewhere else. If you are researching khaprail roof leakage, this guide explains the common causes in plain terms, how to trace them safely, what repairs are usually involved and when to call a professional. It is general guidance, not an inspection of your roof.

---

## What causes khaprail roof leakage?

**Khaprail roof leakage is usually caused by a failure in the roof's detailing or drainage, not by water passing through a sound tile: a cracked or slipped tile, loose ridge or hip bedding, a poorly sealed wall junction, or blocked gutters pushing water back under the tiles.** Clay tile roofs shed water by slope and overlap, so anything that breaks that path can let water in.

## Where should you look first?

Work from the likely spots to the unlikely ones:

1. **Cracked, chipped or slipped tiles.** Wind, falling branches, hail, foot traffic and age can break or move tiles.
2. **Ridge and hip lines.** The bedding or fixings may have loosened or cracked.
3. **Valleys.** Water concentrates here, and debris can block the flow.
4. **Parapet and wall junctions.** Flashing or sealing can crack with movement and heat.
5. **Pipes, chimneys and rooflights.** Anything that cuts through the roof needs careful sealing.
6. **Gutters and downpipes.** If they are blocked, water backs up under the eave tiles.
7. **Slope and overlap errors.** A roof laid too flat, or with too little overlap, may leak in heavy or wind-driven rain.
8. **Underlay or deck.** In some roofs, the layer under the tiles is damaged or was never continuous.

## How do you trace a leak?

Leaks rarely drip directly under the hole. Water can travel along battens, rafters and the underlay before it appears. A careful approach:

- **Start inside.** Note where the stain is, check the loft or roof space with a torch, and look for wet timber and daylight.
- **Trace uphill.** The entry point is usually higher than the stain.
- **Check from outside in dry weather**, from the ground with binoculars or a drone, before going up.
- **Mark suspects** and have a roofer test with a hose from the bottom of the slope upward, one section at a time. Water testing is a task for someone experienced.

Safety first: clay tiles crack under a person's weight and surfaces can be slippery. Unless you have experience, hire a roofer rather than climbing up yourself.

## How are common khaprail leaks repaired?

The right repair depends on the cause, and your roofer should confirm it on site. In general terms:

- **Broken or slipped tiles** are replaced or re-seated. Ask for matching tiles, ideally from the same batch, so the colour blends.
- **Loose ridge or hip tiles** are lifted and re-bedded with fresh mortar or the fixing method your roofer prefers.
- **Valleys and gutters** are cleared and any damaged lining repaired.
- **Wall and parapet junctions** are re-flashed or re-sealed with materials suited to the surface.
- **Pipe and chimney details** are re-sealed or re-flashed.
- **Underlay or deck problems** may need part of the roof lifted for repair.

Coatings and surface sealers are sometimes sold as quick fixes. A surface coating does not correct a slope, overlap or detailing problem, so ask a roofer to diagnose the cause before you pay for one. ## Could the original installation be the problem?

Sometimes. If leaks appear in many places soon after the roof is built, ask whether the slope, overlap, battens and underlay were done to the plan. Our article on [how khaprail roof tiles are installed](/blog/how-khaprail-roof-tiles-are-installed-slope-battens-and-overlap) lists the basics to compare against. Follow the engineer's or architect's specification, and keep any written agreement with your contractor.

## When should you call a professional?

Call a roofer if the stain keeps growing, if more than a few tiles are broken, if ridges or valleys have moved, if water reaches wiring or the ceiling sags, or if you cannot find the source. Ask for a written diagnosis and quote, and keep photos of the damage with the date.

## How can you prevent leaks before the next rains?

A short routine does most of the work:

- **Clear gutters, valleys and downpipes** before the monsoon.
- **Check for broken or moved tiles** after storms.
- **Keep spare tiles** from your batch for quick repairs.
- **Trim branches** that overhang the roof.
- **Inspect ridges and wall junctions** once a year.
- **Look at the ceilings** after heavy rain and act on a stain early.
