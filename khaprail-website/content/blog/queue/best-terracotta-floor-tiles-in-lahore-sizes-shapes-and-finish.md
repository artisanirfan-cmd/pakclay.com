## What are terracotta floor tiles?

**Terracotta floor tiles are fired clay tiles, usually in warm natural red or orange tones, made by shaping clay and baking it in a kiln.** The word terracotta literally means baked earth. The tile body is colour-through, so the tone runs through the piece rather than sitting only on the surface.

Because they are made from natural clay, shade and surface can vary from tile to tile. For many buyers this variation is the appeal. If you want a perfectly uniform floor, say so when you ask for a sample.

## Which size works best on a floor?

There is no single best size. In general terms:

- **Small pieces (4x4, 6x6)** follow curves, suit smaller rooms and give a traditional, handmade look. They have more joints, so more grout lines to clean.
- **Medium pieces (8x8, 9x9)** are a common balance between look and laying time.
- **Large pieces (12x12)** make a space feel more open and have fewer joints, though they need a flatter base.
- **Rectangles** can be laid in running bond or herringbone patterns for a more designed floor.
- **Octagons and star-and-cross shapes** suit feature areas, entrances and verandas more than a whole house.

Think about the room first, then the tile. A small kitchen in 12x12 can look cut up, while a large lounge in 4x4 can look busy.

## Should the tile be glazed, sealed or left natural?

Natural terracotta is porous in general, which is part of its character. Many installers apply a sealer or wax after laying to make cleaning easier and to protect the surface from stains. Which sealer suits which tile is a question for your installer and the supplier, and we do not publish water-absorption figures for our tiles. Ask for guidance in writing.

If you want a sealed, easy-wipe look, ask whether a sealed finish is possible for the product you like. If you prefer the matte, handmade look, plan for sealing and routine care from day one.

## Are terracotta tiles good for Lahore's heat?

Fired clay is a traditional flooring material in hot climates, and many people find it feels cooler underfoot than darker, denser surfaces in summer, though this depends on thickness, sun exposure and the base underneath. Indoors, a terracotta floor is generally comfortable in Lahore's warm months. Outdoors, a sun-baked surface can still get hot at midday, and wet clay can be slippery, so choose texture and drainage with care.

## How do you lay a terracotta floor properly?

Laying is where most problems start, so brief your contractor before work begins:

1. **Prepare a flat, clean, dry base.** An uneven base shows through the finished floor.
2. **Plan the layout** from the centre of the room so cut pieces end up at the edges.
3. **Soak or damp the tiles if your installer advises it**, since dry clay can pull water out of adhesive.
4. **Leave movement joints** where the floor meets walls and across large areas.
5. **Seal after laying**, following the sealer maker's instructions.

Mix tiles from several boxes while laying to blend natural shade variation across the floor.

## What should you check before ordering?

- **Ask for a sample** from the batch you will receive, and look at it in daylight.
- **Confirm dimensions and thickness** on the real piece.
- **Calculate area with a margin** for cuts, breakage and future repairs.
- **Order the whole floor together** so shade matches.
- **Ask about sealing** and which product your installer recommends.
- **Get everything in writing**, including unit, inclusions and date.
