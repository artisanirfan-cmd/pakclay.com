## What are terracotta tiles?

**Terracotta tiles are made from natural clay that is shaped and fired in a kiln, which gives them a warm, earthy colour that runs through the body of the tile.** Because the colour is in the clay itself, small chips and scratches tend to look natural rather than showing a different layer underneath.

## Where in a Peshawar house can you use terracotta?

The same material can do several jobs. In general terms:

- **Courtyards and verandas.** A natural terracotta floor suits the traditional Peshawar courtyard, where shade, sun and the occasional shower all reach the same surface.
- **Indoor floors.** Living rooms, corridors and bedrooms can take terracotta in a plain square or rectangle for a warm, simple look.
- **Kitchen and bathroom walls.** Smaller formats are used as wall tiles. Our guide to [terracotta kitchen wall tiles](/blog/terracotta-kitchen-wall-tiles-ideas-that-handle-heat-and-splashes) covers splash zones and cleaning.
- **Roofs.** Khaprail tiles are fired clay roofing for sloped roofs, such as a sloped roof over a veranda, a terrace room or a farmhouse.

## How does Peshawar's climate affect the choice?

Peshawar generally has hot summers, cooler winters and a mix of dry spells and rain through the year. For tile choice, three practical points follow. We do not publish heat, frost or water-absorption ratings for our tiles, so none of this replaces judging a real sample:

1. **Sun.** Natural terracotta is colour-through, so it tends to age gracefully outdoors. Look at a sample in strong daylight before you commit.
2. **Rain and wet feet.** Clay is porous, so outdoor floors and roofs depend on good laying: correct slope for drainage, proper bedding and sensible jointing. Ask your mason how they plan to handle water.
3. **Cold mornings.** In cool weather, make sure a wet outdoor tile is not left standing in water. Drainage matters more than the tile name.

## Natural terracotta or a glazed finish?

Natural terracotta has an earthy red-brown tone and a matte look. Glazed ceramic and glazed khaprail tiles have a sealed surface and stronger colour. A glazed surface tends to wipe clean more easily, which helps in kitchens and bathrooms, while natural clay tends to suit courtyards and living areas where a traditional look is wanted. ## How do you plan a terracotta floor?

The planning steps are the same in any city:

1. **Measure the floor area** in square feet and note any steps or corners.
2. **Add a margin** for cuts and breakage. Your tiler can tell you how much, as it depends on the layout.
3. **Order from one batch** so colour is consistent across the floor.
4. **Choose adhesive, grout and sealer with your contractor.** Natural terracotta is porous, so ask about sealing, especially in kitchens and outdoor areas.
5. **Lay a few tiles dry first** to see how the pattern and colour variation look together.

Colour varies slightly from piece to piece in handmade or kiln-fired clay. That variation is part of the look, but you should see it on a sample first.
