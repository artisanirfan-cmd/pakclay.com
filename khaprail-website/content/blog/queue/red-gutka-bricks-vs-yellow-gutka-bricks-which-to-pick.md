## Red gutka or yellow gutka: which one should you put on your wall or floor?

Walk through any building-material market in Lahore and you will hear both: "red gutka" for the classic brick-red pieces, and "yellow gutka" or "yellow bricks" for a pale, sandy-gold alternative. Homeowners often ask which is better, as if one were simply stronger. The honest answer is that colour tells you less than you think. This guide compares red gutka bricks and yellow gutka bricks on look, heat, upkeep and sourcing, so you can choose for your house and ask the right questions before you buy.

---

## What is the difference between red and yellow gutka?

**Red gutka tiles and bricks are small clay pieces fired to a warm brick-red colour, while yellow gutka is a paler sandy-yellow piece whose colour typically comes from the clay or mix used and how it is fired.** Both are used for exterior walls, boundary walls, paths and porches, laid as small units in tight rows.

The word "gutka" is used loosely in the trade. It can mean a solid brick-like piece or a thinner facing tile, so two suppliers selling "yellow gutka" may be offering different products. Always ask for the exact size, thickness and material.

## Why are bricks red or yellow at all?

In general, the colour of fired clay depends on the minerals in the clay and the firing. Iron-rich clays tend to turn red or orange when fired in air. Clays with more lime or less iron often burn to a cream or yellow shade. That means colour reflects raw material and kiln conditions, not a guarantee of strength. A well-fired red piece and a well-fired yellow piece can both be sound, and a poorly fired one of either colour can chip or soak up water.

## Which looks better on a house front?

This is a matter of taste and surroundings. Red gutka gives a traditional, warm look that suits older Lahore streets, courtyards and planted gardens, and its colour tends to hide dust and weathering well. Yellow gutka gives a lighter, sunnier face that can make a plain front look brighter, and it pairs easily with white or cream paint. Take a few pieces outside and look at them in morning, midday and evening light, next to your gate, window frames and paint. Colour looks very different in the shop and on the wall.

## Does colour change how hot it gets?

Lighter surfaces usually reflect more sunlight than darker ones, so a pale wall may feel a little cooler to the touch in strong sun. The effect depends on thickness, finish and how much direct sun the surface receives, so do not treat it as a rule. If heat matters, put sample pieces in the sun for an hour and compare them by hand.

## Which is stronger, red or yellow?

Neither colour automatically wins. Strength depends on the clay, how well it is fired, density and thickness. A useful hands-on check is to tap a piece: a well-fired one tends to ring clearly rather than thud. Look at a broken edge for an even colour and a tight texture, and compare how quickly a dry piece soaks up a drop of water. If a supplier will not let you inspect a spare piece, be cautious. ## How do red gutka and yellow gutka compare?

| Point | Red gutka | Yellow gutka |
|---|---|---|
| Typical look | Warm, traditional brick red | Lighter, sandy yellow |
| Strength | Depends on firing and thickness, not colour | Depends on firing and thickness, not colour |
| Heat in sun | May run warmer in direct sun | May reflect more light, but varies |
| Uses | Walls, paths, porches, courtyards | Walls and facades, mostly decorative |
| Colour matching | Batch-to-batch variation common | Batch-to-batch variation common |

These are general tendencies, not test results. A physical sample settles more than a table.

## What should you check before ordering?

- **Ask for the exact size and thickness,** and whether it is a solid brick or a facing tile.
- **Take a physical sample** and view it in your own light.
- **Buy the whole quantity together,** with a few extra for cuts, so colour matches.
- **Decide the use.** Wall facing, floor and driveway each need a different piece.
- **Get the quote in writing,** with the unit and date.
