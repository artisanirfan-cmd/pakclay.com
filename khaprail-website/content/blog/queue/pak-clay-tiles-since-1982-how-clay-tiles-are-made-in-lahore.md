## What are clay tiles?

**Clay tiles are building tiles shaped from natural clay and fired in a kiln until the material hardens into a durable, stone-like body. Khaprail roof tiles, terracotta floor tiles and gutka bricks are all examples, differing mainly in shape, size, finish and use.**

Fired clay is mineral, so it does not rot or burn, and its colour usually runs through the body of the piece rather than sitting only on the surface. That is why chips on natural terracotta tend to look like the rest of the tile. Finishes such as glaze are added on top for particular looks and uses.

## How are clay tiles made, step by step?

Every maker has its own methods, clay mix and equipment, so treat this as the general sequence rather than a description of any one factory floor.

1. **Selecting and preparing the clay.** Raw clay is dug or sourced, then cleaned of stones and large debris. It is usually ground and blended, and water is added until it is workable. The mix matters, because it influences colour, strength and shrinkage.
2. **Shaping.** The prepared clay is formed into the tile shape. Depending on the product this can be done by pressing into a mould, by extruding through a die and cutting, or by hand. Roof tiles with a curved profile and flat floor tiles are shaped differently, and handmade pieces show small variations that machine-made ones do not.
3. **Drying.** Freshly shaped pieces hold a lot of water. They are dried slowly and evenly, because drying too fast can crack or warp a tile before it ever reaches the kiln.
4. **Firing.** Dried tiles are loaded into a kiln and heated to a high temperature, which turns soft clay into a hard ceramic body. Firing time and temperature depend on the clay and the product, and they are a major reason tiles from different makers behave differently.
5. **Glazing, where needed.** Glazed products, such as the green glazed khaprail, get a coating that is fired onto the surface. Natural red tiles are left unglazed.
6. **Sorting and checking.** Fired tiles are inspected, and pieces that are cracked, badly warped or off-size are set aside. A sound tile should ring rather than thud when tapped, a simple check many tile buyers use, though it is no substitute for a supplier's own quality checks.

## Why do tiles from the same batch look slightly different?

Clay is a natural material, and a kiln does not heat every corner identically. Small shade differences, minor size variation and surface texture are normal in terracotta, and many buyers like the look because it feels handmade. If an exact match matters, for instance when replacing a few roof tiles or finishing a floor, ask for tiles from a single batch and keep a few spares. Colour on a phone screen is also unreliable, so a physical sample is the safest way to judge it.
