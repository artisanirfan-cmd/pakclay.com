## What should you bring to the shop?

A little preparation turns a browse into a decision. Bring:

- **Rough measurements** of the floor, wall or roof area, even if they are approximate.
- **Photos of the space,** including the existing floor or roof and the surrounding paint and doors.
- **A paint chip or phone photo of the wall colour,** since terracotta looks different beside cream, white or dark trim.
- **Your use case:** indoor floor, outdoor porch, boundary wall, jali screen or roof.
- **The name of your mason or contractor,** so questions about fixing can be answered together.

If you do not have measurements yet, do not delay the visit. Come and look first; measure afterwards with the clay in mind.

## How do you choose between tile types on the day?

Match the piece to the place before you fall for the colour. In general, terracotta suits floors, verandahs and courtyards where a warm, natural look is wanted; khaprail is the roof piece, available as natural red or glazed styles; gutka and brick-type pieces suit walls and paths; jali is for ventilation and decoration; and ceramic mosaic suits bathroom and kitchen walls. If you want help narrowing a long list, our [categories page](/categories) groups products by use and material.

Ask three questions for every piece you like: What is the exact size and thickness? Is it meant for indoor, outdoor or roof use? What does fixing and sealing involve? Clay is porous, so a good supplier should be comfortable explaining base preparation and the option of a breathable sealer.

## Should you buy on the first visit?

Usually not, and that is fine. The sensible order is to look, take free samples home, test them in your own light and then request a written quote. Take two or three pieces home, place them on the actual floor or against the actual wall, and look at them at morning, noon and evening. Wet one piece to see how the colour deepens, and press a sample against your paint colour. Decisions made in your own room tend to hold up better than decisions made under shop lights.

## How is the price set at a tiles shop?

We do not print a rate on this page, because a number without a unit and a date can mislead you. Tile prices usually depend on size, thickness, shape, finish, quantity, the season and delivery distance, and they may be quoted per piece, per square foot or per thousand. Ask for a written quote that states the unit, what is included and the date it was confirmed.
