## What are khaprail tiles?

**Khaprail tiles are fired clay roofing tiles, made from natural clay and baked in a kiln until hard. They are a traditional sloped-roof covering in Punjab and are closely related to terracotta roof tiles.** The tile sheds rain along its curves or flats and gives the roof a layer of fired clay above the structure.

A tiled roof works as part of a system: structure, slope, underlay, ventilation and tile all matter. We do not promise a fixed temperature drop or a fixed lifespan, because both depend on the whole build and on maintenance.

## What should Gujranwala and Sialkot buyers think about?

Both cities generally have hot summers, cooler winters and a monsoon season with heavy bursts of rain, and Sialkot sits closer to the foothills. That suggests a few priorities:

- **Rain drainage.** Correct slope and overlap are more important than the tile shape, and gutters need to cope with heavy bursts.
- **Even firing.** Evenly fired tiles tend to cope better with repeated wetting and drying.
- **Cold nights.** We do not make frost-resistance claims. If your site gets cold, ask your contractor about the conditions on your roof and request advice from the supplier on your specific tile.
- **Fixing.** Ask how tiles will be fixed to the battens, especially in wind.

## Is a khaprail roof right for a new house or a renovation?

Both are possible, but the planning is different. In a new house you can design the roof slope, battens and drainage with the tile in mind, which is the easiest route. In a renovation, you must check that the existing structure can carry a tiled covering, and whether the slope suits the profile. A flat concrete slab may need a sloped frame before tiling, which is a structural question for an engineer or an experienced roofing contractor. Do not order tiles until that question is answered, and ask for the load and slope advice in writing from the person who will build it.

## What mistakes should you avoid?

- **Ordering by photo only.** Colour on a screen differs from colour on the roof, so always ask for a sample.
- **Mixing batches.** Order the full quantity together to keep the colour consistent.
- **Skipping the underlay and ventilation plan.** The tile is only one layer of the roof.
- **Comparing prices without units.** A per-piece rate and a per-square-foot rate are not comparable until converted.
- **Accepting a verbal quote.** Ask for it in writing with the date.

Avoiding these gives you a much better chance of a roof that looks right and drains well from the first monsoon.
