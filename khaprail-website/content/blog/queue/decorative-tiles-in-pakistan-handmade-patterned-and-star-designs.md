## Which decorative tiles suit a Pakistani home?

Decorative tiles do the work that paint and furniture cannot: they give a wall, a floor or a courtyard a pattern you can see from across the room. In Pakistan, where clay has been fired for centuries, the choice runs from plain terracotta squares to star-and-cross shapes, hexagons, glazed mosaics and pierced jali screens. This guide explains the main kinds of decorative tiles, what "handmade" and "hand painted" usually mean, how Moroccan and star designs differ from local clay work, and how to pick for a floor, a wall or a feature panel. ## What are decorative tiles?

**Decorative tiles are tiles chosen mainly for their shape, pattern, colour or surface finish rather than for plain coverage, and they are used as feature floors, borders, wall panels, backsplashes or screens.** They can be clay, ceramic, stone or cement. The decorative effect usually comes from the tile's cut shape, a glaze, a relief pattern, or the way a layout combines several shapes.

## What do handmade, hand painted and machine-made mean?

These terms get used loosely, so it helps to know what to ask. A handmade tile is shaped, trimmed or finished by hand, which is why edges, size and shade can vary slightly from piece to piece. A hand painted tile carries a design applied by a brush, usually on a glazed surface. A machine-made tile is pressed and finished in a controlled process, so size and colour are more uniform. None of these is automatically better. Handmade clay suits a warm, traditional look and tolerates small variations. Uniform tiles suit a strict grid. Ask the supplier which process a tile follows, then judge a real sample, since photographs can hide shade and texture differences.

## Are star tiles and Moroccan tiles the same thing?

Not quite. Star-and-cross is a geometric pattern: an eight-pointed star set between cross shapes, found in many Islamic building traditions. Moroccan tiles, often called zellige, are a specific craft in which glazed clay pieces are cut by hand and assembled into mosaics. Many of those are made to order in workshops abroad. A star-and-cross terracotta tile from a Lahore clay maker borrows the geometry, but it is a different product with its own finish. Pak Clay does not list Moroccan zellige tiles. ## Which decorative tiles work for floors?

Floors need a tile that handles foot traffic, so shape and finish matter together. Terracotta suits living rooms, verandas and courtyards, and square formats such as [12x12 inch](/products/square-12x12-inch-natural-terracotta-floor-tiles) lay quickly, while hexagons and octagons make the floor itself the feature. Large tiles for the floor mean fewer joints and a calmer look, but they show uneven bases, so the surface must be level. Small tiles, borders and star shapes work well as inlays framed by plain squares. For wet or outdoor floors, choose a tile with grip, and read our [outdoor floor tiles guide](/blog/outdoor-floor-tiles-in-pakistan-terracotta-otti-and-paver-options) before you decide.

## Which decorative tiles work for walls?

Walls allow more delicate tiles, since they carry no foot traffic. Terracotta hexagons add warmth to feature walls and entrances. ## What are the latest tile design trends, and should you follow them?

Pattern layouts, earthy terracotta tones, hexagons and glazed mosaics keep coming back, and so do handmade-look finishes with visible variation. Trends change quickly, though, and a wall tiled in a fashion that dates in five years is costly to redo. A safer approach is a timeless base, such as plain terracotta squares, with the decorative element limited to a border, a single feature wall or a small panel. That keeps the room flexible and cuts the amount of patterned tile you need to buy. We do not rank designs as "latest" or "best", because taste, light and the rest of the room decide that.

## How do you order decorative tiles without regrets?

Start with a sample. Look at it in daylight and under your room lighting, dry and wet, next to your paint colour and furniture. Measure your area and add a wastage margin, which is higher for diagonal layouts and for patterns with many cut pieces. Order everything from one batch so shade stays consistent. Ask whether shade variation is normal for the tile, and whether clay pieces need a sealer. Confirm sizes in writing, because handmade tiles can vary a little. Finally, plan the layout and the border before the mason starts, since decorative tiles are hard to adjust once fixed.
